## Practice questions

**1.** **[Weaken]** The town's library recorded 20% more visitors after it began opening on Sundays. The librarian says Sunday opening is clearly the reason for the increase.

Which of the following would weaken her claim the most?
a) Membership is free.
b) The library also launched a large book sale during the same period.
c) Sundays are quiet in the town.
d) The library is open until 8 p.m.

**2.** **[Inference]** If the new bridge opens before the festival, traffic in the old city will ease. The bridge did not open before the festival.

Which of the following must be true?
a) Nothing can be concluded about the traffic from these statements alone.
b) The festival was cancelled.
c) Traffic in the old city did not ease.
d) Traffic in the old city eased.

**3.** **[Assumption]** Rohan has scored above 80% in each of his last five tests. His teacher expects him to score above 80% in the next one.

The teacher's expectation depends on the assumption that
a) Other students scored lower.
b) The earlier tests were easy.
c) the next test is similar in difficulty to the earlier ones.
d) Rohan studies every day.

**4.** **[Flaw]** A survey of 50 readers of a cooking magazine found that 40 cook at home daily. The editor concludes that most people in the country cook at home daily.

The editor's conclusion is weak mainly because
a) fifty is an odd number.
b) cooking is a hobby.
c) the magazine is expensive.
d) the readers of a cooking magazine may not represent the whole country.

**5.** **[Strengthen]** Since the company introduced flexible working hours, employee satisfaction scores have risen. The manager believes flexible hours are the cause.

Which of the following, if true, most strengthens the manager's belief?
a) Satisfaction surveys are anonymous.
b) Satisfaction rose most in departments that used flexible hours the most.
c) The company also has a canteen.
d) Some employees prefer fixed hours.

**6.** **[Inference]** All the lamps in the hall are off. Some lamps in the corridor are on.

Which of the following must be true?
a) Some lamps that are on are not in the hall.
b) All lamps in the corridor are on.
c) The hall has no lamps.
d) No lamp in the corridor is off.

### ANSWER KEY

1. **b** — A second event (a book sale) offers another explanation for the higher number of visitors.
2. **a** — The statement says what happens if the bridge opens; it says nothing about what happens if it does not (traffic may ease for other reasons).
3. **c** — Predicting from past performance assumes that conditions stay the same, including how hard the test is.
4. **d** — The sample is biased toward people who like cooking; it cannot stand for the whole population.
5. **b** — A dose-response pattern (more flexibility, more satisfaction) points to flexible hours as the cause.
6. **a** — The lamps that are on include corridor lamps, and none of those is in the hall (all hall lamps are off).
