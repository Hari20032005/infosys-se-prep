## 3. Concept notes

### 3.1 How to answer any behavioural question: STAR

Situation (10 s), Task (10 s), **Action (40 s)**, Result (15 s). Use “I” for your actions. A story without a metric is fine; a story with an invented metric is not. A set of ten STAR stories built from your own work is in `IV5a-star-stories.md`; fill the `[YOU FILL IN]` fields truthfully before you rehearse.

### 3.2 The eight standard answers

| Question | What a good answer contains |
|---|---|
| Tell me about yourself | Who you are, your strongest skill (Java), two projects in one line each, one line of motivation; 60 seconds |
| Why Infosys? | Two real reasons (see `00-INFOSYS-SPECIFIC.md`): training at Mysuru, a services career across many clients and technologies, the AI-first direction; not “it is a big company” |
| How do you handle rejection? | A real example, what you did next, what changed; no bitterness |
| How do you manage time? | A method you actually use (list, deadline, review), with one example from a busy exam-plus-project week |
| Why did your CGPA dip? (if true) | One honest cause, what you changed, the recovery since. Your CGPA is 8.07, so if asked about a decline, show the trend in numbers |
| How do you handle stress? | One concrete habit (break the task, sleep, ask for help), one example |
| Will you relocate? | “Yes”, plainly, if it is true. The eligibility text already requires it |
| Job or higher studies? | The truth. If you plan to sit GATE 2027, say what you will do if you receive the offer and what you want from the first years of work. Interviewers test commitment, not a promise to never study |

### 3.3 “Are you willing to work in different technologies?”

The honest evidence-based answer is in `IV5b-technology-flexibility.md`: you choose the stack by fit, you have already picked up TypeScript and Django code for open-source fixes, and you learn by running the code first and reading it before changing it.

### 3.4 Non-verbal basics

Sit upright, answer in short paragraphs, do not interrupt, ask for a question to be repeated rather than guessing. One GfG account (seen in a search snippet) says the interviewer guided candidates who started in the wrong direction: listen for hints `[REPORTED]`.

## 6. Traps and gotchas

| Trap | Why it catches people | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| A rehearsed monologue | Sounds scripted | Answer the question asked, in 60–90 seconds |
| Saying “weakness: perfectionism” | Everyone does | Name a real habit with a fix |
| Denying a dip | Panels see the transcript | Explain and show recovery |
| Saying “yes” to relocation and meaning “maybe” | The offer letter says “any location” | Say yes only if it is true |
| Badmouthing a teammate | Looks like a risk | Describe the situation, not the person |
| Inventing a number | One follow-up breaks it | Say “I did not measure it” |
