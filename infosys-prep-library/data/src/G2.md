## 2. Exactly what gets asked

The English Grammar section is **5 questions, 10 marks, 10 minutes** (2 marks each), built as a **cloze passage**: a short paragraph with five blanks, each with four options `[LIKELY]` (C1: “close test based”; V1 adds the grammar list). The coaches' topic list for it is: tense, articles, prepositions, parts of speech, active–passive voice, punctuation.

| Blank type | What the options test | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Tense / verb form | simple vs perfect vs continuous; agreement | “By the time we arrived, the match ___ started.” (had) |
| Article | a / an / the / no article | “She is ___ honest woman.” (an) |
| Preposition | in / on / at / for / since / by / with / of | “He has lived here ___ 2019.” (since) |
| Conjunction / connector | but / although / because / so that | “___ it rained, the match went on.” (Although) |
| Pronoun and agreement | who/which, each/their, subject–verb | “Each of the players ___ tired.” (was) |
| Voice | active ↔ passive | “The report ___ by Friday.” (will be submitted) |

**Why this section is T1.** It is 10 of the 75 scored marks, each mark is cheap to win, and it has its own cut-off. Five blanks are solvable from five short rules.

## 3. Concept notes

### 3.1 How to solve a cloze passage

1. Read the **whole passage once** without looking at options. You learn the tense and the subject.
2. For each blank, read the **word before and the word after**; most blanks are fixed by one of them.
3. Decide the **type** (tense, article, preposition, connector).
4. Eliminate options that break the type; if two survive, re-read the sentence aloud in your head.

### 3.2 Tenses in four lines

| Tense | Form | Signal words |
|---|---|---|
| Simple present | verb / verb + s | always, every day, usually |
| Present perfect | have/has + past participle | already, yet, just, since, for, so far |
| Simple past | past form | yesterday, ago, last year, in 2019 |
| Past perfect | had + past participle | before, by the time, after (earlier of two past events) |
| Future | will + verb; will have + past participle | tomorrow, by next year |

**since + point in time; for + duration.** *Since 2019, for five years.*

### 3.3 Articles

- **a / an** before a singular, countable, non-specific noun. Use **an** before a **vowel sound**: *an hour, an honest man, a university, a one-rupee coin*.
- **the** for something specific or unique: *the sun, the Ganga, the best*.
- **no article** for plural or uncountable general statements: *Water is essential. Dogs bark.*
- No article with languages, meals, most sports.

### 3.4 Prepositions

| Use | Preposition |
|---|---|
| Point in time | *at* 5 pm, *on* Monday, *in* March, *in* 2019 |
| Duration / starting point | *for* two years, *since* 2019, *from* … *to* |
| Place | *at* the station, *in* Chennai, *on* the table |
| Fixed pairs | good **at**, interested **in**, depend **on**, afraid **of**, different **from**, discuss (no preposition) |

### 3.5 Subject–verb agreement

The verb agrees with its **subject**, not the nearest noun. *The list of items **is** ready.* *Each/every/neither/either* take a singular verb. *One of the boys **is** absent.* Collective nouns (*team, committee*) are singular when they act as one body.

### 3.6 Voice

Active: *The manager approved the plan.* Passive: *The plan was approved by the manager.* Form: **object becomes subject; be-verb in the same tense + past participle.** The passive with *by* is used when the actor is unknown or unimportant.

## 4. Must-memorise block

| Rule | Shortcut |
|---|---|
| since / for | point / length |
| a / an | vowel **sound** |
| had + V3 | earlier of two past events |
| either/neither/each/every | singular |
| “the” | specific or unique |
| passive | be + V3 |

**Irregular participles to know cold:** go–went–gone, see–saw–seen, write–wrote–written, take–took–taken, give–gave–given, begin–began–begun, choose–chose–chosen, break–broke–broken.

## 5. Worked examples

**Example 1 — tense.** “By the time the manager arrived, the team ___ the report.” Options: has finished / had finished / finished / was finishing. *By the time … arrived* marks an earlier completed action → **had finished**.

**Example 2 — article.** “He is ___ honest man and ___ intelligent student.” *honest* starts with a vowel sound (silent h) → **an**; *intelligent* starts with a vowel sound → **an**. Answer: an … an.

**Example 3 — preposition.** “She has been working here ___ 2021.” A starting point → **since**.

**Example 4 — agreement.** “The number of applicants ___ increased.” The subject is *number* (singular) → **has**. Compare: *A number of applicants **have** applied.*

**Example 5 — voice.** “The new software ___ by the team next month.” Future passive → **will be released**.

**Example 6 — a cloze in full.**
“Air pollution (1) ___ become a serious problem in (2) ___ cities. Factories and vehicles release gases (3) ___ harm human health. (4) ___ the government has introduced strict rules, pollution levels are still high. Clean energy (5) ___ the best answer.”
(1) **has** (present perfect, singular subject); (2) **many** / **large** (no article before a plural with “many”); (3) **that** (relative, restrictive); (4) **Although** (contrast); (5) **is** (singular). Read it back: every sentence is grammatical.

## 6. Traps and gotchas

| Trap | Why it catches people | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing “a” before an “h” word | Spelling, not sound, tempts you | Say the word aloud in your head |
| Using “for” with a date | *for 2019* is wrong | Point = since; length = for |
| Agreeing with the nearest noun | *A list of items are…* | Find the real subject |
| Treating “had” as just another past | Two past events need an order | Look for *by the time, before* |
| Fixing one blank without the next | Blanks interlock in a cloze | Read the whole sentence back |
| Skipping the first read | You miss the tense | Always read the passage once first |
