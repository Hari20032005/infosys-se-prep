## 2. Exactly what gets asked

One observed item, a **coded relation** question, plus the classic chain questions every Indian aptitude paper uses `[REPORTED]`. Blood relations is low-effort: the method below takes about half an hour to learn.

| Sub-pattern | How it appears | Observed |
|---|---|---|
| Symbol-coded relations | "X + Y means X is the sister of Y, X − Y means X is the father of Y, X × Y means X is the brother of Y. Which means M is the son of S?" | R13 (reported twice in the same channel) |
| Chain of statements | "P is the son of R. R is the father of Q. How is P related to Q?" | authored from the pattern |
| Pointing at a photo | "Pointing to a man, a woman said, ‘His mother is the only daughter of my mother.’" | authored, classic |

## 3. Concept notes

### 3.1 Draw it

Draw each person as a dot with M or F. Draw **vertical** lines for parent → child, **horizontal** lines for siblings, and a double line for a married couple. Every blood-relation question is then a path between two dots.

### 3.2 Three moves

| Move | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Up | to a parent |
| Down | to a child |
| Side | to a sibling |

A path is a sequence of moves; its name depends on the sequence and on the sex of the destination.

| Path | Name |
|---|---|
| Up | father / mother |
| Down | son / daughter |
| Side | brother / sister |
| Up, Up | grandfather / grandmother |
| Down, Down | grandson / granddaughter |
| Up, Side | uncle / aunt |
| Side, Down | nephew / niece |
| Up, Side, Down | cousin |
| Spouse, Up | father-in-law / mother-in-law |
| Down, Spouse | son-in-law / daughter-in-law |
| Spouse, Side or Side, Spouse | brother-in-law / sister-in-law |

Two shortcuts: *Up then Down* is *Side* (siblings share a parent), and *Side then Up* is *Up* (your sibling's parent is your parent).

### 3.3 Symbol-coded relations (R13)

Translate each symbol into words before anything else. R13: S − P means S is P's father; P + M means P is M's sister; M × G means M is G's brother. So S is the father of P and M is P's sibling, so S is M's father as well; and M is male (a brother). Therefore **M is the son of S**, and the expression `S − P + M × G` is correct.

### 3.4 "Only"

"The only daughter of my mother" is **me** if I am female. "My mother's only son" is me if I am male. "Only" collapses the family down to one person.

### 3.5 Gender rules

The word “brother”, “father”, “son”, “husband” fixes the speaker as male; “sister”, “mother”, “daughter”, “wife” fixes female. A name never fixes gender: use letters and the words only.

## 4. Must-memorise block

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Mother's brother | maternal uncle |
| Father's sister | paternal aunt |
| Uncle's or aunt's child | cousin |
| Sister's husband | brother-in-law |
| Wife's brother | brother-in-law |
| Son's wife | daughter-in-law |
| Husband's mother | mother-in-law |
| Brother's son | nephew |
| Brother's daughter | niece |
| Father's father | paternal grandfather |

## 5. Worked examples

**Example 1 — coded relation (R13).** Above: `S − P + M × G`.

**Example 2 — chain.** “U is the son of R. R is the father of Q. How is U related to Q?” R is the parent of both U and Q, so U and Q are siblings. U is male (son), so U is Q's **brother**.

**Example 3 — chain with an in-law.** “J is the sister of I. I is the wife of C. How is J related to C?” From C: Spouse (I), then Side (J) → sister-in-law. J is female. **Sister-in-law.**

**Example 4 — the photo.** The woman says: “His mother is the only daughter of my mother.” My mother's only daughter is me. So his mother is me: the woman is the man's **mother**.

**Example 5 — three generations.** “A is B's sister. C is B's mother. D is C's father. How is A related to D?” D is C's father; C is A's mother; so D is A's grandfather, and A is D's **granddaughter**.

## 6. Traps and gotchas

| Trap | Why it catches people | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Assuming gender from a name | Names are not fixed in the question | Take gender only from the relation word |
| Mixing maternal and paternal | The question may not ask which | Answer at the level the options use |
| Forgetting that “brother” implies a male | M × G in R13 fixes M as male | Mark M/F on your diagram |
| Answering from the wrong person | “How is A related to D” vs “D to A” | Write the direction in the margin |
| Treating “only” loosely | It collapses to the speaker | Replace “the only daughter of my mother” with “me” if female |
| Reversing the direction of a coded symbol | `+` could mean sister of Y or Y of sister | Write the meaning in words first |
