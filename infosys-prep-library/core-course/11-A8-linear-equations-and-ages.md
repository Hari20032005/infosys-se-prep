# Lesson 11. Linear equations, ages and heads-and-legs

*Topic `A8` · about 1.8 of the 75 marks · plan about 0.7 hours.*

## 1. The idea

A linear equation is a sentence in maths: "some number, plus another, equals this." Word problems about ages, coins, animals' legs and prices all turn into a few of these sentences. You write them down, then solve.

You do not need advanced algebra. You need two moves:

- **Give each unknown a letter** (x for one, y for the other) and write each fact as an equation.
- **Get rid of one letter**, either by adding the equations or by replacing one letter with what it equals.

Once one letter is known, the other follows. Always check the answer by putting it back into the story.

## 2. What Infosys actually asks

Three questions were found in the 2025 reports `[REPORTED]`:

- Two equations, `2x + 3y = 7` and `5x + 8y = 2`, asking which relation holds between x and y. The answer was x > y, with x = 50 and y = −31.
- Ages: the present ages of two people are 48 and 72. Find the ratio of the first person's age three years ago to the second person's age three years from now. The answer is 3 : 5.
- A farm with cows and hens: 45 heads and 130 legs. How many cows? The answer is 20.

These are among the most common word problems in the Technical Ability section, which is 10 questions in 35 minutes, about three and a half minutes each. That is plenty of time if you set the equations up calmly.

## 3. The method

1. **Pick letters.** Write what each letter stands for, such as "x = pens, y = pencils".
2. **Write one equation for each fact.**
3. **Pick a way to remove a letter.**
    - *Add or subtract the equations* when the same letter has matching numbers in front.
    - *Replace* when one equation says "x is …".
    - *Guess and adjust* for heads-and-legs: pretend all are the two-legged animals, then see how many legs are missing.
4. **Solve for the first letter.** Then find the second.
5. **Check in the story.** Do the numbers make sense? Ages cannot be negative.

For ages, remember that everyone gets older by the same amount. If A is 6 years older than B now, A is 6 years older than B in any year.

## 4. Shortcuts and when they fail

**Add the equations.** If two equations have 3 pens + 2 pencils and 2 pens + 3 pencils, adding them gives 5 pens + 5 pencils, so one pen plus one pencil is a fifth of the total. *It fails if the question asks for one item, not the pair.*

**The middle term of consecutive numbers.** For an odd number of consecutive numbers, the middle one is the sum divided by the count. *It fails for an even count*, where the middle falls between two numbers.

**Heads and legs: assume the smaller.** Pretend every animal has two legs. The legs you are short are made up by the four-legged animals, two extra legs each. *It fails if you forget that each four-legged animal adds only the difference in legs* (here 2, not 4).

**Ages: use present and past in one line.** Write each person's age now, and next to it their age at the other time. *It fails when you forget which time the ratio uses.*

## 5. Worked examples

All the answers were checked by trying every possible whole-number pair in code.

**Example 1 (consecutive numbers).** The sum of five consecutive even numbers is 110. What is the smallest?

The middle number is 110 ÷ 5 = 22. The numbers are 18, 20, 22, 24, 26. The smallest is **18**.

**Example 2 (ages and a ratio).** The present ages of Anil and Bina are 36 and 60. Find the ratio of Anil's age 4 years ago to Bina's age 5 years from now.

Anil 4 years ago: 36 − 4 = 32. Bina in 5 years: 60 + 5 = 65. The ratio is **32 : 65**. It cannot be simplified.

**Example 3 (heads and legs).** A farm has cows and hens. There are 30 heads and 84 legs. How many cows?

Pretend all 30 are hens: 30 × 2 = 60 legs. We are short 84 − 60 = 24 legs. Each cow adds 2 legs, so there are 24 ÷ 2 = **12 cows**. (And 18 hens: 48 + 36 = 84.)

**Example 4 (two equations).** 3x + 2y = 19 and 5x − 2y = 5. Which relation holds between x and y?

Add the equations: 8x = 24, so x = 3. Put it in the first: 9 + 2y = 19, so y = 5. Check in the second: 15 − 10 = 5. Yes. Since 3 is less than 5, the relation is **x < y**.

**Example 5 (prices).** 3 pens and 2 pencils cost ₹36. 2 pens and 3 pencils cost ₹34. What is the cost of one pen and one pencil together?

Add the two: 5 pens and 5 pencils cost ₹70. One of each costs 70 ÷ 5 = **₹14**. (A pen costs ₹8 and a pencil ₹6.)

## 6. Traps

- **Mixing up times in an age question.** "4 years ago" means subtract. "5 years from now" means add.
- **Forgetting to check.** Put your answer back in. A negative age is wrong.
- **Solving for the wrong thing.** The question may ask for a pair, a sum or a relation. Re-read it.
- **Dropping a sign.** In 5x − 2y = 5, the minus matters when you add equations.
- **Assuming a whole number.** The answer to a heads-and-legs question must be a whole number. If it is not, you set up the equation wrongly.

### ANSWER KEY

1. **a** — The middle/average term is 150/5 = 30. The numbers are 26, 28, 30, 32, 34, so the largest is **34**.
2. **c** — 40 − 2 = 38; 64 + 4 = 68. Ratio 38 : 68 = **19:34**.
3. **b** — Suppose all 47 are ducks: 47 × 2 = 94. The extra 40 comes from goats, each adding 2. So goats = 40/2 = **20**.
4. **d** — The middle/average term is 138/4 = 34.5. The numbers are 33, 34, 35, 36, so the largest is **36**.
5. **b** — Solve the pair: a pen costs ₹10, a pencil ₹7. Then 2×10 + 4×7 = **₹48**.
6. **c** — Eliminate one variable (multiply and subtract): x = -20, y = 2. So **x < y**.
7. **c** — Suppose all 55 are hens: 55 × 2 = 110. The extra 50 comes from cows, each adding 2. So cows = 50/2 = **25**.
8. **d** — 18 − 3 = 15; 30 + 3 = 33. Ratio 15 : 33 = **5:11**.
9. **a** — Solve the pair: a pen costs ₹6, a pencil ₹4. Then 4×6 + 1×4 = **₹28**.
10. **d** — Eliminate one variable (multiply and subtract): x = 58, y = 8. So **x > y**.
11. **b** — Let B's age 7 years ago be b. Then A's age then was 2b. Now they are b + 7 and 2b + 7, and the sum is 3b + 14 = 56, so b = 14. Now B = 21 and A = 35. A's present age is **35**.
12. **a** — Let B's age 4 years ago be b. Then A's age then was 4b. Now they are b + 4 and 4b + 4, and the sum is 5b + 8 = 28, so b = 4. Now B = 8 and A = 20. B's present age is **8**.
13. **a** — Solve the pair: a pen costs ₹10, a pencil ₹8. Then 1×10 + 1×8 = **₹18**.
14. **c** — Suppose all 23 are cars: 23 × 4 = 92. The excess 16 comes from bikes, each over-counted by 2. So bikes = 16/2 = **8**.
15. **b** — Let B's age 6 years ago be b. Then A's age then was 3b. Now they are b + 6 and 3b + 6, and the sum is 4b + 12 = 44, so b = 8. Now B = 14 and A = 30. A's present age is **30**.

## 8. Recap in 60 seconds

- Name the unknowns. One equation per fact.
- Remove a letter: add or subtract equations, or replace.
- Heads and legs: assume all have fewer legs, then fix the gap.
- Ages: everyone ages the same. Past means subtract, future means add.
- Always check the answer in the story.
