## The recaps

### 1. Pair and figure puzzles

- Every line uses the **same** hidden rule.
- Look at sizes, pick a rule from the ladder, test on line 1, then line 2, then line 3.
- Add or subtract first, then multiply, then squares and cubes, then digits, then joined steps.
- Plus shape: centre = 2 × the sum of the arms. Cube and box: two cubes plus the two small numbers. Zig-zag: digit sum of each neighbour, added.
- One minute with no rule? Move on and come back.

[Open the lesson](01-Z2-pair-and-figure-puzzles.md)

### 2. Number grids and matrices

- Five seconds to decide the kind: rows with a rule, constant total, repeated number, two grids.
- **Check the total first.** Then fit the first row, confirm on the second and third.
- Rule ladder: sum, difference, product, product ± number, square, product ± third number.
- Repeated first two columns? Only n matters. Think triangular numbers and n² + n.
- Two grids? Add every row; the totals make a series.
- Stuck for a minute? Move on.

[Open the lesson](02-Z1-number-grids.md)

### 3. Number and letter series

- Write the **gaps** first. Always.
- Steady gap: add. Growing gap: gaps of gaps. Ratio: multiply. Zig-zag gaps: split odd and even places.
- Letters become numbers (A = 1 to Z = 26). Remember E, J, O, T, Y.
- A term made of parts: solve each part as its own series.
- Check your rule on **every** term before answering.
- Learn your squares to 15 and cubes to 10.

[Open the lesson](03-A2-number-and-letter-series.md)

### 4. Coding and decoding

- Letters become numbers: A = 1 to Z = 26. Anchors: E 5, J 10, O 15, T 20, Y 25.
- Number codes: sum, then average, then odd or even places.
- Word codes: shift (all letters move the same), swap (order changes), reverse (backwards). Reverse and shift can come together.
- Test the rule on the second example before you use it.
- Sentence codes: the word both sentences share matches the code both share.

[Open the lesson](04-A1-coding-and-decoding.md)

### 5. Data sufficiency

- Count the possible answers. **One** is enough. More than one is not.
- Test I alone, then II alone, then together if you must.
- Marks: both alone = **2**; one alone = **1**; only together = **3**; not even together = **4**.
- One equation, two unknowns: usually not enough. A square gives two values unless something fixes the sign.
- You are judging, not solving. Do not waste time finding the answer.

[Open the lesson](05-A6-data-sufficiency.md)

### 6. Syllogisms and logical deduction

- A conclusion **follows** only if it is true in every picture that fits the statements.
- Picture rules: All = inside. Some = overlap. No = apart. Some not = part outside.
- Two "some" statements: nothing follows. All + all: all (and some).
- Opposite conclusions, neither certain alone: **either I or II**.
- Assume every named group has at least one member.
- Official set: pick three where the first two lead to the third.

[Open the lesson](06-A4-syllogisms-and-deduction.md)

### 7. Operator logic and equation balancing

- Find the rule on line 1, confirm on line 2, then use it.
- Order of ideas: one operation, operation then digit work, operation on digits, add a constant.
- Digit-sum: stop after one pass unless the example reduced further.
- Swapped signs: replace every sign first, then BODMAS.
- Interchange: try all six pairs.
- Brackets first when the operator is nested.

[Open the lesson](07-A3-operator-logic.md)

### 8. Blood relations

- Draw a small tree: up is a parent, down is a child, sideways is a sibling or a spouse.
- Mark M or F only when the question says so.
- Walk from the end person to the first person and name the walk.
- Key walks: up-up is a grandparent, up-sideways is an uncle or aunt, sideways-down is a nephew or niece, up-sideways-down is a cousin.
- Coded signs: translate every sign first.
- No sex given? The answer may be "parent" or "cannot be determined".

[Open the lesson](08-A5-blood-relations.md)

### 9. Non-verbal reasoning

- No real Infosys question of this kind has been found. Learn it last.
- Painted cube n × n × n: corners 8, edges 12(n − 2), faces 6(n − 2)², inside (n − 2)³. They add to n³.
- Standard die: opposite faces add to 7.
- Mirror clock: 12:00 minus the time shown.
- Grid squares: 1² + 2² + … + n². Grid rectangles: choose 2 vertical lines times choose 2 horizontal lines.
- Always check a tiny case.

[Open the lesson](09-A21-non-verbal-reasoning.md)

### 10. Data interpretation

- Five calculations: share, change, ratio, average, pie conversion.
- Change is over the **old** value. Share is over the **whole**.
- Take only the numbers you need. Sanity-check the size of the answer.
- Know the fractions: 1/8 is 12.5%, 1/6 is 16.67%, 1/3 is 33.33%.
- Combined percentages come from the totals, not from averaging percentages.

[Open the lesson](10-A7-data-interpretation.md)

### 11. Linear equations, ages and heads-and-legs

- Name the unknowns. One equation per fact.
- Remove a letter: add or subtract equations, or replace.
- Heads and legs: assume all have fewer legs, then fix the gap.
- Ages: everyone ages the same. Past means subtract, future means add.
- Always check the answer in the story.

[Open the lesson](11-A8-linear-equations-and-ages.md)

### 12. Cloze and fill in the blanks

- Two tests for every answer: **correct grammar** and **sensible meaning**.
- Read the whole sentence. Name the job of the gap. Check agreement. Check the clue word. Read it back.
- `since` = point in time, `for` = length, `by` = deadline.
- `an` before a vowel sound. `information` and `advice` have no plural.
- Finished time named: past simple. Still true now: present perfect. Earlier of two past events: past perfect.
- The subject is the main noun, not the noun closest to the verb.

[Open the lesson](12-G1-cloze-and-fill-in-the-blanks.md)

### 13. Tenses, articles and prepositions

- Read the whole passage first. Name the job of each gap.
- Tenses: named finished time = past simple. *Since* + still true = present perfect. Earlier of two past events = past perfect. Future deadline = future perfect.
- Articles: first mention *a/an*, known thing *the*, uncountable no *a*.
- Prepositions: *in* parts of the day, *on* days and surfaces, *since* point, *for* length.
- Connectors: *so* result, *although* contrast, *because* reason.
- Passive = *be* + past participle.

[Open the lesson](13-G2-tenses-articles-prepositions.md)

### 14. Spotting errors and grammar rules

- Four versions, one correct. Find the words that **change** and check only those.
- Checklist: verb and subject, `number of`, uncountable words, time words, pronouns, lists, opening phrases, -ing after a preposition.
- `each`, `neither`, `nobody` are singular. A phrase in between is ignored. With `nor`, the nearer subject wins.
- `since` = point in time. `for` = length.
- Cross out versions you can break with a named rule. Read the last one for sense.

[Open the lesson](14-V2-spotting-errors.md)

### 15. Sentence correction and completion

- Read the whole sentence. Find the job of the marked part.
- Run the checklist: agreement, tense, time words, pronouns, lists, opening phrases, -ing after a preposition.
- Keep the original if it is right.
- Among correct options, pick the **shortest** that keeps the meaning.
- Cut empty phrases (*due to the fact that*) and repeated pairs (*final outcome*).
- Read your choice back into the sentence.

[Open the lesson](15-V3-sentence-correction.md)

### 16. Reading comprehension

- The passage is the only source of truth.
- Read once for the gist, read the question, find the lines, match the options.
- Inference means **must be true**, not might be true.
- Word in context: put each option into the sentence.
- Watch for "always", "never", "all" and "only", and for the "NOT" in a question.

[Open the lesson](16-V1-reading-comprehension.md)

### 17. Critical reasoning

- Argument = reasons + conclusion, with a gap in between.
- Find the conclusion first (so, therefore, should).
- Strengthen fills the gap. Weaken opens it with another explanation. Assumption: what must be true (test with "not"). Inference: what must follow.
- Cross out off-topic and too-strong options.
- "Two things rise together" does not mean one causes the other.

[Open the lesson](17-V6-critical-reasoning.md)

### 18. The essay and the email

- Essay: point, then three supported paragraphs, then close. About 190 words.
- Email: subject, greeting, why, details, request, thanks, close with your name.
- Plan 2 minutes, write 6, check 2.
- Specifics beat adjectives. Short sentences. Cut empty words.
- No text language. No "In today's world".

[Open the lesson](18-W1-essay-and-email.md)

### 19. Loops and trace tables

- Trace table: a column per variable, a new row per change.
- `For (each i from a to b)` includes b. `While` checks first. `Do … While` runs once before it checks.
- `MOD` = remainder. Whole-number `/` drops the fraction.
- Nested loops: add up the inner turns, one outer turn at a time.
- Check the order of `Print` and the change in each turn.
- Re-read what the question asks for.

[Open the lesson](19-P1-loops-and-traces.md)

### 20. Pre- and post-increment

- `++i`: add 1, then use it. `i++`: use it, then add 1. Either way `i` ends one higher.
- Go **left to right**. Use the three-column table: `i` before, value used, `i` after.
- Result = the "value used" column, combined with the signs. Final `i` = start ± the number of `++` and `--`.
- `i = i++` leaves `i` unchanged. `i = ++i` adds 1.
- In a loop, trace two passes before you decide how it ends.

[Open the lesson](20-P4-increment-operators.md)

### 21. Bitwise operators

- `&` both 1, `|` at least one 1, `^` different bits.
- `<<` multiplies by 2 each step. `>>` divides by 2 each step and drops the fraction.
- Order: arithmetic, then shifts, then `&`, then `^`, then `|`.
- Write the binary. Work column by column. Convert back.
- Add brackets in your head for the operator that goes first.
- Do not read left to right.

[Open the lesson](21-P3-bitwise-operators.md)

### 22. Arrays

- Arrays start at index 0. Five boxes: 0 to 4.
- `For (each i from 0 to n-1)` visits every box.
- `step 2` skips one; `step -1` counts down.
- Redraw the row when the program changes a box.
- Running total: each box becomes itself plus the one before.
- Check the first and last index used.

[Open the lesson](22-P5-arrays.md)
