# Lesson 14. Spotting errors and grammar rules

*Topic `V2` · about 5.3 of the 75 marks · plan about 1.2 hours.*

## 1. The idea

In an "identify the correct sentence" question you get four versions of one sentence. Three have a mistake. One is right. You pick the right one.

You do not need to know every rule in English. The four versions are almost the same. They differ in only a few words. So your job is to find the words that change from one version to the next, and check only those words.

Most of the mistakes come from a short list: the verb does not match its subject, the pronoun does not match its noun, the time word does not match the tense, the article is wrong, a list is not in matching form, or an opening phrase hangs in the air. Learn the list and the question becomes a checklist.

## 2. What Infosys actually asks

The mistakes in the wrong versions were the kinds in this lesson: a verb that did not match its subject, a pronoun that did not match its noun, a wrong word form, and a tense that did not fit.

## 3. The method

1. **Read all four versions quickly.**
2. **Find the words that change.** Put a finger on the first place where two versions differ. There are usually one to three such places.
3. **Check those words against the checklist below.**
4. **Cross out every version with a broken rule.**
5. **If two remain, look at the places you did not check.** The last mistake is often in a word that is the same in two versions but different in the others.
6. **Read the one left, once, for sense.**

The checklist:

| Look for | The rule |
|---|---|
| The verb | It matches the real subject, not the noun nearest to it. `each`, `either`, `neither`, `nobody` are singular. With `nor`, match the nearer subject. A phrase between the subject and the verb is ignored. With `there is/are`, match what comes after. |
| `the number of` and `a number of` | `The number of` takes a singular verb. `A number of` means "some" and takes a plural verb. |
| Words with no plural | `information`, `advice`, `knowledge` and `help` have no plural form and take a singular verb. |
| Time words | A named, finished time (yesterday, in 2018) takes the past simple. A state that began in the past and is still true takes the present perfect. `since` goes with a point in time, `for` with a length. |
| Pronouns | They match their noun in number, and do not switch person. |
| A list | The items have the same form (all -ing, or all the same kind of phrase). |
| An opening phrase | The person doing the action in the phrase must be the subject that follows. |
| After a preposition | The verb takes its -ing form (for arriving, not for arrive). |

## 4. Shortcuts and when they fail

**Find the odd one out.** If three versions agree on a word and one differs, that word is often the clue. *It fails when the odd one is itself correct*, so check it.

**Cross out the verb mismatches first.** They are the quickest to spot. *It fails when the verb is far from its subject.* Find the subject first.

**Count the endings.** If you see `-s` on both the noun and the verb (`students are`, but `student are`), check whether the pair fits. *It fails with words that do not change in the plural*, such as `sheep`.

**Trust your ear, but check.** A wrong version often sounds odd. *It fails because many wrong versions sound normal in casual speech.* Test every one against the checklist.

## 5. Worked examples

These are judgement questions. Each shows the correct sentence and why each wrong version fails.

**Example 1 (there is and there are).** Which is correct?

- "There are many questions in the paper." (Correct.)
- "There is many questions in the paper." *Many questions* is plural, so *is* is wrong.
- "There are a question in the paper." *A question* is singular, so *are* is wrong.

**Example 2 (a word with no plural).** Which is correct?

- "She gave me good advice." (Correct.)
- "She gave me good advices." *Advice* has no plural form.
- "She gave me a good advices." Same problem, and *a* cannot go with a plural.

**Example 3 (since and the present perfect).** Which is correct?

- "I have lived in Mysuru since 2023." (Correct.)
- "I live in Mysuru since 2023." *Since* with a state that began earlier needs the present perfect.
- "I am living in Mysuru since 2023." Same problem.

**Example 4 (a list).** Which is correct?

- "He enjoys cooking, singing and dancing." (Correct.)
- "He enjoys cooking, singing and to dance." The last item does not match the first two.
- "He enjoys to cook, singing and dancing." The first item does not match the others.

**Example 5 (an opening phrase).** Which is correct?

- "Walking to the station, Ravi saw an old friend." (Correct.)
- "Walking to the station, an old friend was seen." Who was walking? It cannot be the friend.
- "Walking to the station, the morning was cool." The morning cannot walk.

## 6. Traps

- **Matching the verb to the nearest noun.** In "The box of pencils is on the table", the subject is *box*.
- **Trusting a wrong sentence that sounds normal.** Check the checklist anyway.
- **Skipping the unchanged words.** Sometimes the mistake is in a word that is the same in two versions.
- **Changing your mind on a hunch.** Change an answer only if you can name the rule.
- **Forgetting that `the number of` and `a number of` differ.** One is singular, the other plural.


## 7. Practice questions

Attempt each level before opening the key. Level 3 is harder than anything in the 2020 official sample, on purpose: if you can do Level 3, the real paper will feel easy.

### Level 1: warm-up

*One clear rule per question.*

**1.** **[Agreement]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) Each of the boy has a bag.  
b) Each of the boys has a bag.  
c) Each of the boys are carrying a bag.  
d) Each of the boys have a bag.  

**2.** **[Time words]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) She has lived here since five years.  
b) She has lived here during five years.  
c) She has lived here for five years.  
d) She lives here since five years.  

**3.** **[Tense]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) I met him yesterday.  
b) I am meeting him yesterday.  
c) I have met him yesterday.  
d) I meet him yesterday.  

**4.** **[Article]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) He is an honest a man.  
b) He is honest a man.  
c) He is a honest man.  
d) He is an honest man.  

**5.** **[Uncountable noun]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) The informations you gave me were useful.  
b) The information you gave me was useful.  
c) The information you gave me were useful.  
d) The informations you gave me was useful.  

### Level 2: exam level

*Phrases between the subject and the verb, nor, the number of, pronouns and lists.*

**6.** **[Agreement]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) The manager, along with his assistants, is attend the meeting.  
b) The manager, along with his assistants, are attending the meeting.  
c) The manager, along with his assistants, are attended the meeting.  
d) The manager, along with his assistants, is attending the meeting.  

**7.** **[Agreement]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) Neither the teacher nor the students were present.  
b) Neither the teacher nor the students was present.  
c) Neither the teachers nor the student were present.  
d) Neither the teacher nor the students is present.  

**8.** **[Number of]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) The number of applicants have increased this year.  
b) A number of applicants has increased this year.  
c) The numbers of applicant has increased this year.  
d) The number of applicants has increased this year.  

**9.** **[Pronoun agreement]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) The girl brought their lunch box.  
b) The girls brought her lunch boxes.  
c) The girls brought their lunch boxes.  
d) The girls brought its lunch boxes.  

**10.** **[Parallel structure]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) She likes read, writing and painting.  
b) She likes reading, writing and painting.  
c) She likes reading, writing and to paint.  
d) She likes to read, writing and painting.  

### Level 3: beyond the exam

*Opening phrases, person shifts, tense with two events, -ing after a preposition, and a sentence where three words change at once.*

**11.** **[Dangling modifier]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) After finishing the report, it was sent to the client.  
b) After finishing the report, Meera sent it to the client.  
c) Having finished the report, the email was sent by Meera.  
d) After finishing the report, the client received it.  

**12.** **[Pronoun agreement]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) When people are tired, they make more mistakes.  
b) When people are tired, it makes more mistakes.  
c) When people are tired, one make more mistakes.  
d) When people are tired, you make more mistakes.  

**13.** **[Tense]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) By the time we reached the hall, the show starts.  
b) By the time we reached the hall, the show has started.  
c) By the time we reached the hall, the show had started.  
d) By the time we reached the hall, the show will start.  

**14.** **[Gerund after a preposition]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) She apologised to arriving late.  
b) She apologised for arrive late.  
c) She apologised for arriving late.  
d) She apologised for to arrive late.  

**15.** **[Combined]** Identify the grammatically correct sentence.  
a) Small habits on their own have the power to change a career, whatever their size.  
b) Small habits on their own has the power to change a career, whatever their size.  
c) Small habits on their own have the power to changing a career, whatever its size.  
d) Small habits on its own have the power to change a career, whatever their size.  

### ANSWER KEY

1. **b** — *Judgement:* *Each* is singular, so the verb is **has**. The other options use a plural verb with *each* or leave *boy* in the singular after *of the* (Purdue OWL, subject-verb agreement).
2. **c** — *Judgement:* Five years is a length of time, so **for**. *Since* needs a starting point such as a year or a day (Purdue OWL, prepositions of time).
3. **a** — *Judgement:* *Yesterday* names a finished time, so the past simple **met** is used. The present perfect does not go with a stated time (British Council, present perfect).
4. **d** — *Judgement:* The *h* in *honest* is silent, so the word starts with a vowel sound and takes **an** (Purdue OWL, a versus an).
5. **b** — *Judgement:* *Information* is uncountable: no *s*, and the verb is singular, **was** (Purdue OWL, uncountable nouns).
6. **d** — *Judgement:* The subject is *the manager*. The phrase *along with his assistants* sits in between and does not change the verb: **is attending** (Purdue OWL, a phrase between subject and verb).
7. **a** — *Judgement:* With *nor*, the verb agrees with the nearer subject. *Students* is plural, so **were**. In the last option the nearer subject is *student*, which needs *was* (Purdue OWL, subject-verb agreement).
8. **d** — *Judgement:* *The number of* takes a singular verb, as in the dictionary's example *The number of homeless people has increased*. *A number of* means *some* and takes a plural verb (Oxford Learner's Dictionaries, *number*).
9. **c** — *Judgement:* A pronoun agrees in number with its noun: plural *girls* takes **their** (Purdue OWL, using pronouns clearly).
10. **b** — *Judgement:* The three items must have the same form. All three are -ing words here (Purdue OWL, parallel structure).
11. **b** — *Judgement:* The opening phrase names an action, so the person who did it must be the subject that follows. Only *Meera* finished the report (Purdue OWL, dangling modifiers).
12. **a** — *Judgement:* Do not shift person or number in one sentence: *people* is third person plural, so the pronoun is **they** (Purdue OWL, using pronouns clearly).
13. **c** — *Judgement:* Two past events: the show starting came before we reached the hall. The earlier one takes the past perfect, **had started** (Purdue OWL, active verb tenses).
14. **c** — *Judgement:* After the preposition *for*, the verb takes its -ing form, **arriving**. A gerund can be the object of a preposition (Purdue OWL, gerunds).
15. **a** — *Judgement:* The subject *habits* is plural, so **have**; the pronouns *their* match it. The other options mix *has*, *its* and *to changing* (Purdue OWL, agreement and pronouns).

## 8. Recap in 60 seconds

- Four versions, one correct. Find the words that **change** and check only those.
- Checklist: verb and subject, `number of`, uncountable words, time words, pronouns, lists, opening phrases, -ing after a preposition.
- `each`, `neither`, `nobody` are singular. A phrase in between is ignored. With `nor`, the nearer subject wins.
- `since` = point in time. `for` = length.
- Cross out versions you can break with a named rule. Read the last one for sense.
