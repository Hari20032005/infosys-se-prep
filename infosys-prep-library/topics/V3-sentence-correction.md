## 3. Concept notes

### 3.1 Subject-verb agreement

The verb agrees with the **head noun of the subject**, not with the nearest noun.

Rule set:

- **Ignore prepositional phrases** between subject and verb. *The box of chocolates **is** on the table.*
- **Each, either, neither, everyone, everybody, anyone, nobody, somebody, one** are singular. *Each of the students **has** a book.*
- **Both, few, many, several** are plural. *Several of the boys **are** absent.*
- **Either/neither ... or/nor** → verb agrees with the **nearer** subject. *Neither the manager nor the clerks **were** present.*
- **Not only ... but also** → agrees with the nearer subject too.
- **Collective nouns** (team, jury, committee, family) are singular when acting as a unit, plural when members act individually.
- **"A number of"** → plural verb. **"The number of"** → singular verb.
- **Titles, distances, sums of money** take a singular verb. *Twenty kilometres **is** a long walk.*
- **There/Here** sentences: verb agrees with what follows. *There **are** three reasons.*
- **Relative pronoun** (who/which/that) as subject takes the verb of its antecedent. *He is one of the students who **work** hard.*
- Words ending in **-s** that are singular: news, physics, mathematics, economics, politics, measles, billiards.

### 3.2 Tense and sequence

- Two past actions: the **earlier** one goes in past perfect. *He **had left** before I arrived.*
- Past perfect needs a **second past event** or a time marker. Never use it alone.
- **Universal truths** stay in the present even after a past reporting verb. *He said the sun **rises** in the east.*
- After **since** (time), the main clause is present perfect or past perfect. *I **have lived** here since 2019.*
- After **for** (duration) with present relevance → present perfect.
- **Time clause with future meaning** takes present tense: *When he **comes**, I will tell him.* Not "will come".
- **No future in if-clauses** for real conditions: *If it **rains**, we will cancel.*

### 3.3 Conditionals and subjunctive

| Type | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Zero | If + present, present | If you heat ice, it melts. |
| First | If + present, will + V | If it rains, we will cancel. |
| Second | If + past, would + V | If I **were** rich, I would travel. |
| Third | If + had + V3, would have + V3 | If I had studied, I would have passed. |

**Were** is used for all subjects in the second conditional and after *wish*, *as if*, *as though*, *suppose*. *I wish I **were** taller.*

Verbs of demand, suggestion, recommendation, insistence, proposal take **base form**: *He suggested that she **leave** early.* *The doctor insisted that he **rest**.*

### 3.4 Pronouns

- **who** = subject; **whom** = object. Test: replace with he/him.
- Pronoun agrees with antecedent in **number, gender, person**. *Every student must bring **his or her** book.*
- **One ... one's**; **one ... he** is wrong.
- **Between you and me** (object form after a preposition).
- **than/as** comparisons: complete the clause mentally. *She is taller than **I** (am).*
- **Reflexive** only when subject and object are the same person. *He hurt **himself**.*
- **Each other** = two; **one another** = more than two.

### 3.5 Articles and determiners

- **a** before consonant *sound*: a university, a European, a one-rupee coin.
- **an** before vowel *sound*: an hour, an honest man, an MBA, an X-ray.
- **the** for unique things (the sun), superlatives (the best), ordinals, rivers/seas/groups of islands, and second mention of a countable noun.
- **No article** with proper names, languages, meals, most diseases, and abstract nouns in general use.
- **few** = not many (negative); **a few** = some (positive); **the few** = all that exist.
- **little** = hardly any; **a little** = some.

### 3.6 Parallelism

Items joined by **and, or, but, not only...but also, either...or, neither...nor** must be in the same grammatical form.

*She likes **swimming**, **hiking**, and **reading**.* Not "swimming, hiking, and to read".

Comparisons must compare like with like: *The climate of Delhi is hotter than **that of** Mumbai* — not "than Mumbai".

### 3.7 Modifiers

A modifier attaches to the **nearest** noun.

Wrong: *Walking down the road, a dog bit me.* (The dog was not walking.)
Right: *Walking down the road, I was bitten by a dog.*

**Only** should sit immediately before what it modifies. *Only he gave me a rupee* ≠ *He gave me only a rupee.*

### 3.8 Prepositions and fixed collocations

| Correct | Wrong |
|---|---|
| **comply with** | comply to |
| **discuss** (no preposition) | discuss about |
| **married to** | married with |
| **superior to** | superior than |
| **prefer to** | prefer than |
| **capable of** | capable to |
| **insist on** | insist for |
| **deprived of** | deprived from |
| **abide by** | abide with |
| **different from** | different than |
| **angry with** (a person) | angry on |
| **angry at** (a thing) | — |
| **consist of** | consist in/with |
| **congratulate on** | congratulate for |
| **aware of** | aware about |
| **listen to** | listen (nothing) |
| **reach** (no preposition) | reach to |

### 3.9 Redundancy and wordiness

Delete words that repeat meaning: *return back*, *repeat again*, *discuss about*, *more better*, *most best*, *free gift*, *final outcome*, *advance planning*, *close proximity*, *new innovation*.

Also: **one of the + plural noun** (*one of my **friends***, not friend); **cope up with** → **cope with**; **revert back** → **reply**.

## 4. Must-memorise block

**Subject-verb:** Each/either/neither/everyone/nobody = **singular**. Both/few/many/several = **plural**. Either-or/neither-nor → **nearer subject**. "A number of" = **plural**; "the number of" = **singular**. News/physics/mathematics/economics = **singular**.

**Conditionals:** If + present → will. If + past → would. If + had + V3 → would have + V3. **"If I were"** always, never "if I was" in hypotheticals.

**Subjunctive verbs** (suggest, demand, insist, recommend, propose, request) → **base form**: "suggested that he go".

**Pronouns:** who = subject, whom = object. Between you and **me**. Each other = 2, one another = 3+.

**Articles:** a university, a one-rupee coin, an hour, an honest man, an MBA, an X-ray.

**Prepositions to drill:** comply **with**, discuss (nothing), married **to**, superior **to**, prefer **to**, capable **of**, insist **on**, deprived **of**, abide **by**, different **from**, aware **of**, congratulate **on**, consist **of**, listen **to**, reach (nothing).

**Redundant pairs to kill:** return back, repeat again, discuss about, more better, free gift, revert back, cope up with, one of my friend(s) → friends.

**Tense:** two pasts → earlier in past perfect. After since → present perfect. Future time clause → present tense.

## 5. Worked examples

**Example 1.** Neither the supervisor nor the workers ___ aware of the change.
a) was b) is c) were d) has been

Working: Neither...nor takes the verb of the **nearer** subject. Nearer subject = "workers" (plural). Plural past/present → **were**. Answer **c)**. The slow method of parsing the whole sentence semantics wastes 40 seconds; the nearer-subject rule fires in 5.

**Example 2.** Identify the error: *The number of (A)/ accidents in the city (B)/ have declined (C)/ this year (D).*
a) A b) B c) C d) D

Working: "**The number of**" is singular → verb must be **has declined**. The error is C. Answer **c)**. If the sentence had said "A number of accidents ... have declined", C would be correct — that is the trap.

**Example 3.** If I ___ you, I would accept the offer.
a) was b) were c) am d) had been

Working: Second conditional hypothetical → **were** for all subjects. Answer **b)**. "Had been" would need a third conditional ("would have accepted").

**Example 4.** Which part is wrong? *Walking through the market (A)/ a purse (B)/ was found by me (C)/ lying on the ground (D).*
a) A b) B c) C d) D

Working: The introductory participial phrase "Walking through the market" must modify the subject of the main clause. The main clause subject is "a purse", which cannot walk. Correct version: "Walking through the market, I found a purse lying on the ground." The error is at C because the passive construction creates the dangling modifier. Answer **c)**.

**Example 5.** He suggested that she ___ the proposal before signing.
a) reviews b) reviewed c) review d) will review

Working: Verbs of suggestion take the **base form** in the that-clause. Answer **c)**. Note "reviewed" would be the British "should review" pattern, not offered here; the base form is the standard tested answer.

## 6. Traps and gotchas

| Trap | Why it catches people | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Distractor phrase between subject and verb | "The list of items **are** long" sounds fine | Cross out everything between subject and verb before deciding |
| "A number of" vs "the number of" | Both look like the same construction | Memorise: **a number of = plural**, **the number of = singular** |
| "One of the + noun" | People write "one of my friend" | **One of the** always takes a plural noun |
| Collective noun ambiguity | Team/jury can take either verb | Singular if acting as one unit, plural if members differ |
| Dangling modifier | The sentence reads smoothly | Check the noun right after the comma can perform the action |
| Superficially parallel list | "swimming, hiking, and to read" | Check every list item has the same form |
| "If I was" | Extremely common in speech | In hypotheticals write **were** |
| "discuss about", "revert back" | Standard Indian-English usage | Delete the preposition/redundant word |
| Comparative with double marker | "more better", "most best" | One marker only |
| Past perfect used alone | "He had gone to Delhi yesterday" | Past perfect needs a second past event or a reference point |

## 7. Practice questions

**1.** Each of the participants ___ given a certificate.
a) were b) are c) have been d) was

**2.** She is one of the few candidates who ___ fluent in French.
a) is b) was c) are d) has been

**3.** He ___ in Chennai since 2015.
a) lives b) has been living c) is living d) lived

**4.** The teacher asked the students to ___ the topic in detail.
a) discuss about b) discuss over c) discuss on d) discuss

**5.** If she ___ harder, she would have cleared the cut-off.
a) studied b) studies c) had studied d) would study

**6.** Neither of the two answers ___ correct.
a) are b) were c) is d) have been

**7.** The manager, along with his subordinates, ___ attending the review.
a) are b) is c) were d) have been

**8.** Which part contains the error? *He is (A)/ more better (B)/ than his brother (C)/ at mathematics (D).*
a) B b) A c) C d) D

**9.** Complete the sentence: Hardly had he sat down ___ the bell rang.
a) than b) when c) then d) that

**10.** The committee ___ divided in its opinion on the new policy, and the members ___ still arguing.
a) were, are b) were, is c) was, is d) was, are

**11.** Identify the correct sentence.
a) The climate of Pune is better than Mumbai.
b) The climate of Pune is more better than Mumbai.
c) The climate of Pune is better than Mumbai's climate is better.
d) The climate of Pune is better than that of Mumbai.

**12.** Not only the students but also the teacher ___ surprised by the result.
a) was b) have been c) were d) are

### ANSWER KEY

**1. d)** "Each" is singular → **was**. "Participants" is inside the prepositional phrase and does not control the verb.

**2. c)** The antecedent of "who" is "candidates" (plural), so the relative-clause verb is **are**.

**3. b)** "Since" + a starting point requires present perfect continuous → **has been living**.

**4. d)** "Discuss" takes no preposition → **discuss**.

**5. c)** Third conditional: If + had + V3, would have + V3 → **had studied**.

**6. c)** "Neither of" is singular → **is**.

**7. b)** "Along with his subordinates" is parenthetical; the subject is "The manager" (singular) → **is**.

**8. a)** "More better" is a double comparative; it should be **better**.

**9. b)** "Hardly had ... **when**" is a fixed correlative pair. "Than" pairs with "no sooner".

**10. a)** The committee is divided → members act individually → plural **were**; "members" is plural → **are**.

**11. d)** Comparison must be like with like; "that of Mumbai" stands for "the climate of Mumbai".

**12. a)** Not only...but also takes the verb of the nearer subject — "the teacher" (singular) → **was**.
