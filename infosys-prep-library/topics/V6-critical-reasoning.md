## 2. Exactly what gets asked

“Critical reasoning” is named in the 2025 pattern deck under Verbal Ability (C1) `[REPORTED]`. No observed item was recovered; the tier comes from the section floor. Questions are a short argument (2–4 sentences) followed by one of: strengthen, weaken, assumption, inference/conclusion.

| Question type | What to do |
|---|---|
| Assumption | Find the unstated bridge that the argument needs |
| Strengthen / weaken | Pick the option that makes the conclusion more or less likely |
| Conclusion / inference | Pick what must follow from the facts, nothing more |
| Flaw | Find the gap (correlation → causation, sample too small, generalisation) |

## 3. Concept notes

### 3.1 Break the argument down

**Premise** (what is stated as fact) + **Conclusion** (what the author wants you to accept) + **Assumption** (the unstated link). Find the conclusion first; look for *so, therefore, thus, clearly*.

### 3.2 The four question types

- **Assumption.** Ask “what must be true for the conclusion to follow?” Test each option by *negating* it: if the argument collapses when the option is false, it is the assumption.
- **Strengthen.** The best option adds support for the link between premise and conclusion.
- **Weaken.** The best option attacks the link, offers a competing explanation, or shows a counter-example.
- **Inference.** Stay inside the passage. An inference is **necessarily true**; a stretch is wrong.

### 3.3 Wrong-answer patterns

Too extreme (*always, never, all*); irrelevant (true but unrelated); out of scope (outside the passage); reversed (opposite of what is asked).

## 4. Must-memorise block

| Type | Question stem | Right option looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Assumption | “assumes” / “depends on” | A bridge statement that would break the argument if false |
| Strengthen | “supports” | New information that raises the conclusion's likelihood |
| Weaken | “undermines” | New information that offers another cause |
| Inference | “follows from” | A statement guaranteed by the passage |

## 5. Worked examples

**Example 1 — assumption.** “The company introduced free coffee, and sales rose that quarter. Therefore free coffee raised sales.” *Assumption:* no other factor caused the rise. Negation test: if a festival season caused the rise, the argument fails. ✓

**Example 2 — weaken.** “Students who eat breakfast score higher marks, so breakfast improves marks.” *Weakens:* students who are more disciplined both eat breakfast and study more. (A third cause.)

**Example 3 — inference.** “All trainees who passed the test were offered jobs. Ravi was not offered a job.” *Must follow:* **Ravi did not pass the test.** (“Ravi failed the test” is also correct; “The test was unfair” does not follow.)

## 6. Traps and gotchas

| Trap | Why it catches people | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing a true statement that is irrelevant | True ≠ relevant | Ask: does it connect premise to conclusion? |
| Over-reading an inference | Adds facts not in the passage | Keep only what must be true |
| Missing the conclusion | The stem follows a different sentence | Underline “therefore”, “so”, “clearly” |
| Mixing weaken and strengthen | The stem is easy to misread | Read the last line of the question first |
| Picking extreme words | Always/never rarely right | Prefer cautious statements |

## 7. Practice questions

More questions: [`QB-V6-critical-reasoning.md`](../question-banks/QB-V6-critical-reasoning.md).

**1.** **[Assumption]** The company introduced free coffee in its offices, and sales rose that quarter. Therefore free coffee raised sales.

Which of the following is an assumption on which the argument depends?
a) Employees drink coffee every day.
b) Coffee is the most popular drink in offices.
c) Sales always rise in the first quarter.
d) No other factor explains the rise in sales.

**2.** **[Weaken]** Students who eat breakfast score higher marks. So eating breakfast improves marks.

Which of the following most weakens the argument?
a) Marks depend on the school's syllabus.
b) Students who are disciplined both eat breakfast and study more.
c) Some students skip breakfast and still score high.
d) Breakfast is served in most schools.

**3.** **[Inference]** All trainees who passed the test were offered jobs. Ravi was not offered a job.

Which of the following must be true?
a) The test was unfair to Ravi.
b) No trainee was offered a job.
c) Ravi did not pass the test.
d) Ravi did not attend the test.

**4.** **[Strengthen]** A city reduced its speed limit from 60 to 40 km/h. The number of accidents fell by a third over the next year. Officials say the lower speed limit saved lives.

Which of the following would most strengthen their claim?
a) Nearby cities that did not change their limits saw no fall in accidents.
b) Some drivers ignore the new limit.
c) More people now use public transport.
d) The city planted trees along its main roads.

**5.** **[Flaw]** A store puts up a sign: ‘Buy one, get one free – all items.’ A customer argues that the store must therefore be losing money on every sale.

The customer's argument is flawed because it
a) ignores that the store may have raised prices before the offer.
b) uses a sign as evidence.
c) compares two different stores.
d) assumes that customers always buy two items.

**6.** **[Inference]** Most employees who take public transport to work arrive on time. Meera arrives on time every day.

Which conclusion is best supported?
a) Meera takes public transport.
b) Employees who drive are always late.
c) Meera lives near her office.
d) Meera may take public transport, but the passage does not prove it.

**7.** **[Weaken]** Sales of electric scooters doubled last year. A manufacturer concludes that customers now prefer electric scooters to petrol ones.

Which of the following, if true, most weakens the conclusion?
a) The manufacturer also sells helmets.
b) Fuel prices are expected to fall.
c) Sales of petrol scooters also rose by a third last year.
d) Electric scooters are quiet.

**8.** **[Assumption]** Every software project at the firm that used daily stand-up meetings finished on schedule. The firm's new project will use daily stand-up meetings.

Which of the following is an assumption needed to conclude that the new project will finish on schedule?
a) All teams like stand-up meetings.
b) Daily stand-up meetings were a reason the earlier projects finished on time.
c) Stand-up meetings last less than fifteen minutes.
d) The new project is smaller than earlier ones.

### ANSWER KEY

1. **d** — The conclusion needs the coffee to be the cause. If something else (a festival season) raised sales, the argument fails. Negate the correct option: if another factor did explain it, the argument collapses.
2. **b** — A third cause (discipline) can explain both, so the link between breakfast and marks is undermined.
3. **c** — If passing guaranteed a job, no job means no pass. The other options add facts that the passage does not give.
4. **a** — A comparison city with no change and no fall in accidents supports the limit as the cause.
5. **a** — Losing money is not guaranteed by a free item if the original price was raised to cover it.
6. **d** — ‘Most’ does not mean ‘all’. Arriving on time is consistent with many other reasons. The cautious conclusion is the only safe one.
7. **c** — If petrol scooter sales also rose, a preference for electric ones is not shown; the market may simply have grown.
8. **b** — The argument treats the meetings as the cause of timely completion. Without that link the past pattern proves nothing for the future.
