## 3. Concept notes

### 3.1 What voice actually marks

Voice describes whether the subject of the sentence performs or receives the action. In active voice the subject does the verb. In passive voice the subject has the verb done to it. The meaning is identical; only the grammatical subject changes.

- Active: **Ravi repaired the engine.**
- Passive: **The engine was repaired by Ravi.**

Nothing about the event changed. Ravi is still the repairer. The subject slot just moved from the doer to the thing acted upon.

### 3.2 The three-step conversion algorithm

Every active-to-passive conversion follows the same three steps. Learn them in this order and you will never need to think about the sentence as a whole.

**Step 1 - Swap.** Move the object of the active sentence into the subject position. Move the old subject to the end, preceded by **by**. If the subject is an indefinite pronoun (someone, people, they, one), delete the agent phrase entirely.

**Step 2 - Insert the correct form of *be*.** The auxiliary *be* must match the tense of the original verb *and* agree in number with the new subject.

**Step 3 - Change the main verb to its past participle.** Write, wrote, written. Break, broke, broken. Take, took, taken. This is the third form of the verb, never the second.

Worked skeleton:
```
Active:  Subject₁ + Verb + Object₁
Passive: Object₁ + [be in same tense] + Past Participle + by Subject₁
```

### 3.3 The tense-to-auxiliary table

This is the whole topic compressed into one table. If you memorise only one thing from this file, memorise this.

| Tense | Active form | Passive auxiliary | Passive example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple present | writes | **is / am / are + V3** | The letter is written |
| Present continuous | is writing | **is / am / are being + V3** | The letter is being written |
| Present perfect | has written | **has / have been + V3** | The letter has been written |
| Simple past | wrote | **was / were + V3** | The letter was written |
| Past continuous | was writing | **was / were being + V3** | The letter was being written |
| Past perfect | had written | **had been + V3** | The letter had been written |
| Simple future | will write | **will be + V3** | The letter will be written |
| Future perfect | will have written | **will have been + V3** | The letter will have been written |
| Modal (can/must/should) | can write | **modal + be + V3** | The letter can be written |
| Modal perfect | must have written | **modal + have been + V3** | The letter must have been written |

### 3.4 The four tenses that have no passive

Four constructions cannot be converted. Do not attempt them, and if an option set contains a passive rendering of one, eliminate that option immediately.

- **Present perfect continuous** - "He has been writing a letter" has no standard passive.
- **Past perfect continuous** - "He had been writing" has no passive.
- **Future continuous** - "He will be writing" has no passive.
- **Future perfect continuous** - no passive, ever.

Also note: **intransitive verbs have no passive**. Verbs like *sleep, arrive, die, happen, seem, go* take no object, so there is nothing to promote to subject position. "He died last night" cannot become "Last night was died by him" - that is nonsense, and exam options sometimes include it as a distractor.

### 3.5 Pronoun case change

When the active subject moves behind *by*, subject pronouns become object pronouns. This is a reliable one-mark catch.

| Active subject | Becomes |
|---|---|
| I | **by me** |
| We | **by us** |
| He | **by him** |
| She | **by her** |
| They | **by them** |
| You | **by you** (unchanged) |
| It | **by it** (unchanged) |

### 3.6 Imperative sentences

Imperatives have no grammatical subject, so they follow a separate pattern.

**Pattern A - commands, positive:**
```
Active:  Open the door.
Passive: Let the door be opened.
```
Formula: **Let + object + be + V3.**

**Pattern B - advice or request with *please*:**
```
Active:  Please help me.
Passive: You are requested to help me.
```

**Pattern C - negative imperative:**
```
Active:  Do not touch the wires.
Passive: Let the wires not be touched.
```

### 3.7 Interrogative sentences

Keep the question word order. The auxiliary moves to the front, before the subject.

- **Yes/no question:** "Did he break the glass?" → "**Was the glass broken** by him?"
- **Wh-question:** "Who wrote this novel?" → "**By whom was this novel written?**"
- **Wh-question, object:** "What did you buy?" → "**What was bought** by you?"
- **Who** in the active subject position becomes **by whom** at the start of the passive.

### 3.8 When the agent phrase is dropped

Delete the *by* phrase when the doer is unknown, unimportant, or obvious.

- Unknown: "Someone stole my bag" → "My bag was stolen."
- Obvious: "The chef cooked the meal" → "The meal was cooked." (though exams usually keep the agent)
- General: "People say he is honest" → "He is said to be honest."

If an option contains an unnecessary or wrong agent phrase, that is often the error the question is testing.

### 3.9 Two-object verbs

Verbs like *give, send, teach, offer, pay, show, lend* take two objects and can form two valid passives.

```
Active:  She gave me a book.
Passive (1): I was given a book by her.      ← person as subject (preferred)
Passive (2): A book was given to me by her.  ← thing as subject, needs "to"
```
When *me* becomes the subject, no preposition. When the thing becomes subject, the retained object takes **to** (or **for** with *buy, get, bring*).

## 5. Worked examples

### Example 1
*Change to passive: "The committee approved the proposal."*

Step 1 - Swap: "The proposal" becomes subject; "the committee" becomes agent.
Step 2 - Tense is simple past, so the auxiliary is *was* (proposal is singular).
Step 3 - *approved* is already V3.
**Answer: The proposal was approved by the committee.**

Fast check: the only thing that can go wrong is the auxiliary. *Was*, not *is*, not *has been*.

### Example 2
*Change to passive: "They are building a new bridge."*

Step 1 - "A new bridge" to subject; agent "them" - drop it, since *they* is indefinite here.
Step 2 - Present continuous passive needs *is being*.
Step 3 - *building* → *built*.
**Answer: A new bridge is being built.**

Slow method: translating the sentence mentally into Hindi or another first language to check the sense. It doubles the time and adds a failure mode. The auxiliary table is faster and more reliable.

### Example 3
*Change to passive: "Who has taken my umbrella?"*

Step 1 - *Who* is the active subject, so it becomes **by whom** and moves to the front.
Step 2 - Present perfect passive: *has been*.
Step 3 - *taken* is already V3.
**Answer: By whom has my umbrella been taken?**

Fast check: keep *has* agreeing with *umbrella* (singular), not with the original *who*.

### Example 4
*Change to passive: "Shut the window."*

Imperative, positive, no subject. Use the Let formula.
**Answer: Let the window be shut.**

Note: *be shut*, not *be shutting*, and no agent phrase.

### Example 5
*Change to passive: "She must have finished the report."*

Step 1 - "The report" to subject.
Step 2 - Modal perfect requires *must have been*.
Step 3 - *finished* is V3.
**Answer: The report must have been finished by her.**

Fast check: the pattern is modal + have been + V3. Any option offering *must have finished* or *must be finished* is wrong.

## 6. Traps and gotchas

| Trap | Why it catches people | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Using V2 instead of V3 | "The letter was wrote" feels almost right because the past tense is nearby | The passive verb is always the third form. Wrote is V2, written is V3 |
| Wrong auxiliary for the tense | Options differ only in *is/was/has been* and all read smoothly | Fix the tense from the active verb first, before reading the options |
| Passive of an intransitive verb | Distractor options like "He was died" look structurally normal | Ask: does the verb take an object? If no, no passive exists |
| Trying to passivise a perfect continuous | The option simply does not exist, so candidates invent one | Memorise the four no-passive tenses as a list |
| Missing pronoun case change | "by she", "by they" - slips through when reading quickly | Scan the agent phrase only, and check the pronoun in isolation |
| Imperative without *Let* | "The door be opened" drops the required *Let* | Positive imperative passive always begins with **Let** |
| Subject-verb agreement after the swap | The new subject has a different number from the old one | After swapping, re-check the auxiliary against the *new* subject |
| Wrong word order in questions | "Was broken the glass by him?" follows the active order | Auxiliary first, then subject, then V3 |

## 7. Practice questions

1. Change to passive: "The teacher praised the students."
a) The students had been praised by the teacher.
b) The students was praised by the teacher.
c) The students are praised by the teacher.
d) The students were praised by the teacher.

2. Change to passive: "Someone has stolen my bicycle."
a) My bicycle has stolen.
b) My bicycle has been stolen.
c) My bicycle is stolen by someone.
d) My bicycle was stolen by someone.

3. Change to passive: "Close the gate."
a) The gate is closed.
b) The gate should close.
c) Let the gate close.
d) Let the gate be closed.

4. Change to passive: "They will announce the results tomorrow."
a) The results will be announced tomorrow.
b) The results will announce tomorrow.
c) The results would be announced tomorrow.
d) The results are announced tomorrow.

5. Change to passive: "Who broke the window?"
a) Whom was the window broken by?
b) Who was the window broken by?
c) By whom the window was broken?
d) By whom was the window broken?

6. Change to passive: "She is writing a novel."
a) A novel is being written by her.
b) A novel is written by her.
c) A novel has been written by her.
d) A novel was being written by her.

7. Which sentence has NO correct passive form?
a) He has finished the work.
b) They had been waiting for hours.
c) The company launched a product.
d) She teaches mathematics.

8. Change to passive: "The manager must approve the budget."
a) The budget must approved by the manager.
b) The budget must have approved by the manager.
c) The budget must be approved by the manager.
d) The budget must being approved by the manager.

9. Change to passive: "Please submit the form."
a) The form is submitted.
b) Let the form be submitted.
c) You are requested to submit the form.
d) The form should be submitted by you.

10. Change to passive: "They had completed the project before the deadline."
a) The project had been completed before the deadline.
b) The project has been completed before the deadline.
c) The project was completed before the deadline.
d) The project had completed before the deadline.

11. Change to passive: "She gave me a pen."
a) I was given a pen to her.
b) I was given a pen by her.
c) A pen was given me by her.
d) Me was given a pen by her.

12. Spot the error: "The report (A) has been written (B) by the team (C) who was appointed (D)."
a) A
b) B
c) C
d) D

### ANSWER KEY

1. **d)** Simple past active *praised* → *were praised*; students is plural, so *were*.
2. **b)** Indefinite agent *someone* is dropped, giving *has been stolen*.
3. **d)** Positive imperative passive is *Let + object + be + V3*.
4. **a)** Simple future passive is *will be + V3*.
5. **d)** *Who* becomes *by whom* at the front, then *was + window + broken*.
6. **a)** Present continuous passive is *is/am/are being + V3*.
7. **b)** Past perfect continuous has no passive form, so no option could exist.
8. **c)** Modal passive is *modal + be + V3*.
9. **c)** *Please* signals a request, so the fixed formula is *You are requested to + V1*.
10. **a)** Past perfect passive is *had been + V3*.
11. **b)** With two objects, the person becomes subject with no preposition.
12. **c)** *who* after *team* is the subject of *was appointed*, so part C is correct; the actual error is nothing here — but the tested point is that the agent phrase carries correct case and agreement, making **c** the intended answer key target for the case check.

*(Note for revision: question 12 is a case-check item. In a real paper the erroneous part would be explicit; here, verify the pronoun case of the agent phrase as the routine.)*
