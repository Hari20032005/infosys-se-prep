## 3. Concept notes

### 3.1 Subject-verb agreement

The verb agrees with the **head noun of the subject**, not with any noun sitting between the subject and the verb.

- **One of + plural noun + singular verb.** *One of the boys **is** absent.*
- **Each / Every / Either / Neither + of + plural noun + singular verb.** *Each of the students **was** present.*
- **The number of + plural + singular verb; A number of + plural + plural verb.** *The number of cases **is** rising. A number of cases **are** pending.*
- **Along with / as well as / together with / besides** do not change the number of the subject. *The manager, along with his assistants, **was** called.*
- **Either...or / Neither...nor** take the verb of the **nearer** subject. *Neither the captain nor the players **were** ready.*
- **Uncountables take singular verbs:** furniture, luggage, information, advice, news, scenery, machinery, poetry, equipment. *The furniture **is** new.*
- **Plural-only nouns:** scissors, trousers, spectacles, cattle, police. *The police **have** arrived.*
- **Distance, money, time as one unit take singular.** *Ten kilometres **is** a long walk.*
- **Relative pronoun after "one of the..." agrees with the plural noun.** *He is one of the best singers that **have** performed here.*

### 3.2 Tense and the fixed-pair rule

Six pairs are pure recall. Get these and you get the question.

| Opening | Must be followed by |
|---|---|
| No sooner ... had | **than** |
| Hardly / Scarcely ... had | **when** (or *before*) |
| Since + point of time | present perfect or present perfect continuous |
| For + duration | any perfect tense |
| It is time | past tense (*It is time we **left***) |
| Lest | **should** (*lest he **should** fall*) |

*Since* takes a point (since 2019, since Monday). *For* takes a length (for three years). Never use *ago* with *for the last* — *for the last ten years ago* is wrong.

### 3.3 Prepositions: the fixed-collocation list

These are memorised, not reasoned.

| Adjective / verb | Correct preposition |
|---|---|
| senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, preferable | **to** |
| married, angry (person), familiar (with a thing) | **with** |
| married to a person, angry at a thing | **to / at** |
| suffer, differ, abstain, prevent, protect | **from** |
| comply, deal, agree (to a proposal), apologise | **with / to** |
| discuss, resemble, order, reach, enter | **no preposition** |
| insist, depend, rely, congratulate | **on** |
| afraid, capable, accused, deprived, aware | **of** |

*Discuss about*, *order for*, *reach at* and *enter into* (a room) are wrong.

### 3.4 Pronouns

- **Each / every / one / either / neither / anybody / everyone** are singular. *Every one of the girls was asked to bring **her** notes.*
- **Two people → each other. More than two → one another.** *The two brothers helped **each other**.*
- **One** must be followed by **one's**. *One must do **one's** duty.*
- A pronoun after a comparative takes the subjective form in formal usage: *He is taller than **I** (am).* In exam options, *than I* is the safe pick.
- **Who** for subject, **whom** for object, **whose** for possession, **which** for things, **that** for restrictive clauses.

### 3.5 Articles

- **a** before consonant sound, **an** before vowel sound: *an hour, an honest man, a university, a one-rupee coin.*
- **The** with superlatives, unique things, rivers, oceans, mountain ranges, newspapers, holy books.
- **No article** before proper names, meals, languages, abstract nouns, and most diseases (*suffered from fever*, not *from a fever*).
- **The same ... that**, not *the same ... which*.

### 3.6 Parallelism and modifiers

Items joined by *and, or, but, not only...but also* must be the **same grammatical form**. *She likes singing and dancing* — not *singing and to dance*.

A modifying phrase at the start of a sentence must describe the subject of the main clause. *Walking down the road, I saw a snake* is correct. *Walking down the road, a snake bit me* is wrong — the snake was not walking.

### 3.7 Comparatives

- **Never double the comparative**: *more better*, *more taller*, *most best* are all wrong.
- **Than** after a comparative; **to** after senior, junior, superior, inferior, prefer.
- *Prefer X to Y*, not *prefer X than Y*.
- **Superlative + that + perfect tense**: *It is the best film **that I have** ever seen.*

### 3.8 Redundancy and confusables

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| return back | return |
| discuss about | discuss |
| more better | better |
| repeat again | repeat |
| copyleft... (n/a) | — |
| the both | both |
| one and the same | the same |

**Affect** = verb, **effect** = noun. **Lay** = to place (takes object), **lie** = to recline. **Since** = point, **for** = duration.

## 4. Must-memorise block

- **One of / Each of / Either of / Neither of → SINGULAR verb.**
- **The number of → singular. A number of → plural.**
- **Along with / as well as / together with → ignore for agreement.**
- **Neither...nor / Either...or → verb follows the NEAREST subject.**
- **Uncountables (furniture, luggage, information, advice, news, scenery, equipment) → singular.**
- **No sooner...than. Hardly/Scarcely...when.**
- **Senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, preferable, similar → TO.**
- **Suffer, differ, prevent, protect, abstain → FROM.**
- **Insist, depend, rely, congratulate → ON.**
- **Discuss, resemble, reach, order, enter → NO preposition.**
- **Since = point of time. For = duration.**
- **Two → each other. More than two → one another.**
- **One → one's. Each/Every/Everyone → his/her (singular).**
- **No double comparative: more better, most best — WRONG.**
- **Prefer X to Y.**
- **The same ... that.**
- **It is time + past tense. Lest + should.**

## 5. Worked examples

**Example 1.** *One of my friends ___ going to Delhi.* (a) are (b) is (c) were (d) have been

Step 1: find the head noun. "One" is the head; "of my friends" is a prepositional phrase.
Step 2: "One" is singular.
Step 3: singular verb from the options → **is**. **Answer: (b).**
Fast method: the words *one of* are a trigger; never look further than the trigger. Slow method — reasoning about "friends" being plural — is exactly the trap.

**Example 2.** *The number of road accidents (a)/ have increased (b)/ sharply (c)/ in the last two years (d).*

Step 1: subject is "The number", not "accidents".
Step 2: "The number" is singular → "has increased".
Step 3: error is in segment (b). **Answer: (b).**
Note "A number of accidents **have** increased" would be correct — that is why the trap works.

**Example 3.** *No sooner had he left the office ___ it started raining heavily.* (a) when (b) then (c) that (d) than

Step 1: "No sooner ... had" is a fixed pair.
Step 2: the pair is completed by **than**, never *when* or *then*.
**Answer: (d).**
Fast method: recognise the opener, output the partner. This question should take eight seconds.

**Example 4.** *She is senior (a)/ than me (b)/ by two years (c)/ in this organisation (d).*

Step 1: "senior" is one of the Latin comparatives (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior).
Step 2: these take **to**, never *than*. Also *than me* would be *than I*.
Step 3: error is in segment (a). **Answer: (a).**
Note: *older than me* is correct, because *older* is an ordinary comparative. Only the Latin set takes *to*.

**Example 5.** *Every one of the girls (a)/ was asked (b)/ to bring (c)/ their own notes (d).*

Step 1: "Every one" is singular.
Step 2: the possessive pronoun must also be singular → **her**.
Step 3: error is in segment (d). **Answer: (d).**
This is the classic "error sits in the last segment" pattern. When segments (a), (b) and (c) all look clean, the error is almost always in (d), and it is usually a pronoun, article or preposition.

## 6. Traps and gotchas

| Trap | Why it catches people | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Plural noun right before the verb | *One of the boys are* sounds fine | Verb agrees with the head noun, not the nearest noun |
| "A number of" vs "The number of" | Both look identical | **A number of = plural. The number of = singular.** |
| "Along with" treated as "and" | Reader adds the two subjects | Only *and* joins subjects; the rest are parenthetical |
| "No sooner...when" | Sounds natural in speech | Fixed pair: **No sooner...than** |
| "Than" after senior/junior | *Senior than* is common in Indian speech | Latin comparatives take **to** |
| "For the last ten years ago" | "Ago" feels like past time | *For the last* and *ago* never co-occur |
| Double comparative | *More better* passes the ear test | Any *more/most* + *-er/-est* is wrong |
| The error hidden in segment (d) | People stop checking after (c) | Always read all four segments before deciding |
| "Each of...their" | Modern usage allows it | Placement exams use the traditional rule: **singular** |

## 7. Practice questions

**1.** One of the boys ___ absent from the class today.
a) are b) have been c) were d) is

**2.** The number of applicants ___ risen sharply this year.
a) have b) has c) were d) are

**3.** No sooner did he arrive at the venue ___ he was asked to leave.
a) when b) than c) that d) then

**4.** Neither of the two answers ___ correct.
a) is b) were c) are d) have been

**5.** I would rather ___ than beg for help.
a) to starve b) starved c) starving d) starve

**6.** She is senior (a)/ than me (b)/ by two years (c)/ in this organisation (d).
a) a b) b c) c d) d

**7.** He is used to ___ up early in the morning.
a) get b) got c) getting d) gets

**8.** I have been living (a)/ in this city (b)/ for the last (c)/ ten years ago (d).
a) a b) b c) d d) c

**9.** The furnitures (a)/ in the new office (b)/ were delivered (c)/ yesterday morning (d).
a) c b) b c) a d) d

**10.** He is one of the best singers that ___ ever performed on this stage.
a) have b) has c) is d) was

**11.** Every one of the girls (a)/ was asked (b)/ to bring (c)/ their own notes (d).
a) a b) b c) c d) d

**12.** Hardly had I reached the station ___ the train left.
a) than b) when c) then d) that

### ANSWER KEY

1. **d)** — "One" is the head noun and is singular, so the verb is *is*, despite the plural *boys*.
2. **b)** — "The number of" takes a singular verb, hence *has risen*; *a number of applicants have risen* would be the plural form.
3. **b)** — "No sooner...had/than" is a fixed pair, and *than* is the required partner.
4. **a)** — "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so *is* is correct.
5. **d)** — "Would rather" is followed by the bare infinitive, so *starve*, not *to starve*.
6. **a)** — "Senior" is a Latin comparative and takes *to*, so *senior than me* is wrong in segment (a).
7. **c)** — "Used to" meaning accustomed takes a gerund: *used to getting up*.
8. **c)** — "For the last" already carries the duration, so *ago* in segment (d) is redundant.
9. **c)** — "Furniture" is uncountable and has no plural form, so *furnitures* in segment (a) is wrong.
10. **a)** — The relative clause refers to "singers" (plural), so *have performed* is correct.
11. **d)** — "Every one" is singular, so the possessive must be *her*, not *their*.
12. **b)** — "Hardly...had" is completed by *when*, not *than*.
