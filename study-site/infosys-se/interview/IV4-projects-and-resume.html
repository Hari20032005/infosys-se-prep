IV4 — Projects and resume

Tier 2 · Interview · interview stage · about 1.0 h · confidence Medium

1. Why this topic matters here

This is interview material, not test material. 2 questions on this theme were reported across 2 first-hand interview accounts (B2, B5), so it is Tier 2 for the interview stage, 1.0 hours to prepare. Interviews are face to face on campus, 15–40 minutes, technical and HR usually in one conversation (../research/03-pattern-synthesis.md §6). The questions are at textbook level; the teaching body below is sized to answer them aloud, not to pass a written exam.

2. Exactly what gets asked

Project questions were asked in two of five first-hand Infosys interview accounts [REPORTED]: “Please explain your final-year project in detail” (B2) and “Explain your capstone project” (B5). A third candidate (B4) credits “the technical skills I mentioned in my resume” for answering well, so expect resume-driven questions too. The interview is 15–40 minutes, so expect one project to be chosen and probed for 5–10 minutes.

Sub-pattern How it appears Observed
“Explain your final-year / capstone project” Open prompt, then follow-ups B2, B5
“Tell me about a project or skill on your resume” Panel picks one from the resume B4 (implied)
“Why this technology?” Choice of stack, database, model common follow-up
“What would you improve?” Checks honesty and depth common follow-up

3. Concept notes

3.1 The five-part project answer (90 seconds)

Part What to say Time
Problem Who has the problem and why it matters 15 s
Approach The design in two or three sentences 25 s
Your part What you built, in “I” sentences 25 s
Result What works, measured or shown; say “not measured” if it is not 15 s
Improvement One honest thing you would do differently 10 s

3.2 Choose the project by the panel's likely interest

Infosys hires for services: applications, testing, maintenance, databases (Systems Engineer: “application development, testing, and maintenance”, per the drive pages). A project with a database, an API and a front end will be discussed comfortably. Lead with one of those.

If you worked on this codebase, these are real defects you can discuss as a code reviewer (from your context file §2.6). Say only what you have checked yourself in the code: 1. The auth middleware sets req.user.id but many controllers read req.user._id, so some writes fail validation. 2. Account suspension is never enforced at login or in the middleware. 3. 3.5 Questions a services panel asks about any project

Why that database? How does authentication work? What happens if two users click at the same time? How did you test it? How is it deployed? What would break first at 100× the users? Have a one-sentence answer for each of your three projects.

5. Worked examples

Example 1 — RAG, 90 seconds. “Researchers often need one fact from a long paper. My project lets you upload a paper and ask a question, and the answer comes back with the page and the exact chunk it used, so you can check it. I built it on FastAPI with a Chroma vector store and a sentence-transformer model; uploads are processed in the background so the page does not time out. I added a section-tree layer that steers a question like ‘what dataset did they use’ towards the experiments section. I have not measured retrieval quality yet; the next step would be a labelled question set and recall@k.” (No percentages, no “multi-turn”.)

Example 2 — Sugarcane, 90 seconds. “Farmers in our region lose crops to two pests. Our team of four built a system that combines a photo of the plant, analysed by a YOLOv8 model, with a questionnaire the farmer fills in; the two scores are blended. We won first prize at a DBT-sponsored hackathon. [My part: YOU FILL IN.] We did not measure accuracy on a held-out set, and the blend weights were set by judgement, not tuned.”

Example 3 — “Why MongoDB for WasteZero?” “The data is document-shaped: opportunities with a list of required skills, applications, notifications. Mongoose gave us schema validation. For pickups and payments at scale, a relational database would be safer because those need transactions.”

Example 4 — “How do you stop duplicate applications?” “A compound unique index on (opportunity, volunteer), so the database rejects duplicates even if two requests arrive together.”

6. Traps and gotchas

Trap Why it catches people How to avoid it
Claiming a repository you did not author The link is public and the interviewer is at the company that ran the programme Name your module; do not claim the repo
Listing every project 15 minutes is not enough Offer two and let them pick
A percentage with no evaluation behind it One follow-up exposes it Say “not measured”
Saying “we” for everything They cannot credit you “I” for your actions, “we” for context
A dead live demo A broken link looks careless Test the URLs the day before
Reading the resume aloud Wastes the time Give the story, not the bullets

7. Practice questions

These are spoken drills. Answer aloud, in 60–90 seconds, then compare with the outline.

  1. Explain your RAG project in 90 seconds.
  2. Explain the Sugarcane system. What was your part?
  3. Which module of WasteZero did you build?
  4. Why did you choose ChromaDB?
  5. How does JWT authentication work in WasteZero?
  6. How do you prevent duplicate applications?
  7. How did you test your project?
  8. What was the hardest bug, and how did you find it?

Model answers (outlines)

  • 1. Problem (finding one fact in a long paper) → approach (chunk, embed, retrieve, cite) → your part (solo) → result shown, not measured → one improvement (evaluation set, real multi-turn).
  • 2. Two inputs (image + questionnaire), YOLOv8 plus a fusion score, team of four, hackathon prize; your own module named specifically; say accuracy was not measured.
  • 3. A module name, the milestone, how it connected to the rest; if the consolidated repo belongs to a teammate, say so first.
  • 4. Embedded, no separate service, free-tier friendly; at scale you would use pgvector or a managed store.
  • 5. Signed token with id and role in an httpOnly cookie, a protect middleware reading it, role guards; then the localStorage weakness you found.
  • 6. A compound unique index on (opportunity, volunteer): the database enforces it even under concurrent requests.
  • 7. Only what is true: pytest for the RAG backend and its CI; say which parts had no tests.
  • 8. A real bug: symptom, how you reproduced it, where it lived, the fix, how you know it is fixed.
