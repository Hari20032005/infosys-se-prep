1. The idea

A blood-relations question gives a few facts about who is related to whom. You have to say how two people are related. It is like reading a small family tree.

The facts come as a chain: "P is the father of Q. Q is the sister of R." Your job is to walk along the chain and name the link between the two people at the ends.

There are only three moves in a family tree. You can go up (to a parent), down (to a child), or sideways (to a brother, a sister, a husband or a wife). Every relation is a short walk made of those moves. A father's brother is "up, then sideways", and that is an uncle.

Two facts keep you safe. First, the sex of a person is only known when the question says it or when the word says it (father, sister, son and so on). Second, a name gives no hint about sex. The questions use letters for that reason.

2. What Infosys actually asks

One blood-relation question was reported in 2025, and it used signs in place of words [REPORTED]. It said that "x + y" means x is the sister of y, "x − y" means x is the father of y and "x × y" means x is the brother of y. It then asked which expression means "M is the son of S". The answer was S − P + M × G.

There was no blood-relation question in the official 2020 sample. This topic has only one reported question, so it is a short topic with a low cost, about half an hour.

The Reasoning section is 15 questions in 25 minutes, about 100 seconds each. These questions are quick if you draw the tree.

3. The method

  1. Write each fact as a short line. For example "P → father of Q" means P is Q's father.
  2. Draw the people as small boxes in rows, one row for each generation: grandparents on top, then parents, then children.
  3. Join the boxes with the facts. A vertical line is parent and child. A horizontal line is brother, sister or husband and wife.
  4. Mark the sex of every person you can (M or F), from the words used.
  5. Start from the person named at the end and walk to the person named first. Count your moves: up, down, sideways.
  6. Name the walk. The table below gives the usual names.
  7. If the sex of the person is not known, say so. "Parent" is a valid answer. "Father" is not, unless the sex is given.

Common walks:

Walk Name
up, up grandfather or grandmother
up, sideways uncle or aunt
sideways, down nephew or niece
up, sideways, down cousin
husband or wife, then sideways brother-in-law or sister-in-law
sideways, then husband or wife brother-in-law or sister-in-law
husband or wife, then up father-in-law or mother-in-law
down, then husband or wife son-in-law or daughter-in-law

4. Shortcuts and when they fail

"Your parent's brother is your uncle; your parent's sister is your aunt." Learn the whole table of walks once. It fails if you mix the sexes, so mark them on the tree.

A sibling of a sibling is a sibling. A brother's sister is your sister, as long as they have the same parents. It fails for half-brothers and half-sisters, which exams almost never use.

For coded signs, translate first. Write what each sign means beside the chain. It fails if you read a sign backwards. "x − y means x is the father of y" has x as the father, not y.

When a person's sex is missing, the answer may not exist. If every option names a specific sex but the question does not give it, the answer is "cannot be determined", if that option exists. It fails when a word in another fact gives the sex. Check.

5. Worked examples

The family relations below were checked by a program that follows the same up, down and sideways rules.

Example 1. P is the father of Q. Q is the sister of R. How is P related to R?

Q and R are brother and sister, so they have the same father. P is the father of Q, so P is also the father of R. Answer: father.

Example 2. A is the brother of B. B is the mother of C. How is A related to C?

From C, go up to B (the mother). From B, go sideways to A (her brother). Up, then sideways, is an uncle. A is male because he is a brother. Answer: uncle.

Example 3 (the sex is missing). A is the brother of B. B is the son of C. How is C related to A?

A and B are brothers. C is the parent of B, so C is also the parent of A. But the question does not say whether C is a man or a woman. Answer: parent (father or mother, not known).

Example 4 (a cousin). A is the daughter of B. B is the brother of C. C is the mother of D. How is A related to D?

From D, go up to C, go sideways to B, go down to A. Up, sideways, down is a cousin. Answer: cousin.

Example 5 (the reported style with signs). "x + y" means x is the sister of y, "x − y" means x is the father of y and "x × y" means x is the brother of y. Which expression means M is the son of S? Take S − P + M × G.

Read it: S is the father of P. P is the sister of M. M is the brother of G. So P and M are brother and sister. S is the father of both of them. M is a brother, so M is male. M is the son of S. Answer: S − P + M × G.

6. Traps

  • Guessing the sex from a letter or a name. Use only what the question says.
  • Reading the chain in the wrong direction. "A is the son of B" has A at the bottom of the tree.
  • Stopping halfway. The answer links the two ends, not the middle people.
  • Mixing up sides. A mother's brother and a father's brother are both uncles. Their names differ in some languages but not in these questions.
  • Forgetting the in-law walks. A wife's brother and a sister's husband are both brothers-in-law.

8. Recap in 60 seconds

  • Draw a small tree: up is a parent, down is a child, sideways is a sibling or a spouse.
  • Mark M or F only when the question says so.
  • Walk from the end person to the first person and name the walk.
  • Key walks: up-up is a grandparent, up-sideways is an uncle or aunt, sideways-down is a nephew or niece, up-sideways-down is a cousin.
  • Coded signs: translate every sign first.
  • No sex given? The answer may be "parent" or "cannot be determined".
