MOCK-01 — Answers and working

Mark each answer, add up the sections, and compare with the targets. Cut-off values for the real test are not known; one coach says roughly 70% per section applied in the previous year [UNVERIFIED]. Use 70% per section as a safe target.

Section 1 — Reasoning Ability

  1. b — Compare the code with the word letter by letter: odd positions keep the alphabet number and even positions become 0. SILVER → 19012050. Apply it to JUNGLE: J=10 U=0 N=14 G=0 L=12 E=0 → 100140120.
  2. d — Compare the code with the word letter by letter: even positions keep the alphabet number and odd positions become 0. SMART → 0130180. Apply it to FLOWER: F=0 L=12 O=0 W=23 E=0 R=18 → 012023018.
  3. b — The code is the average of the letter positions. LAST: 52 ÷ 4 = 13; NORTH: 75 ÷ 5 = 15. ENGINE: 54 ÷ 6 = 9.
  4. c — The terms are consecutive primes: 13, 17, 19, 23, 29 → the next prime is 31, giving 31.
  5. b — The terms are consecutive primes: 17, 19, 23, 29, 31 → the next prime is 37, giving 37.
  6. c — Three tracks. Numbers: 8, 15, 24, 35, 48, next 63. Letters step back two places each time: V, T, R, P, N, next L. Last numbers rise by 4: 6, 10, 14, 18, 22, next 26. Answer 63L26.
  7. c — each term is the sum of the two before it: 10 and 16 give 26.
  8. b — Divide, then add the digits of the quotient once. 536 ÷ 8 = 67 → 13; 198 ÷ 2 = 99 → 18. 630 ÷ 6 = 105 → 6.
  9. c — Swap each sign for what it stands for first: 5 − 7 − 9 + 10 becomes 5 + 7 + 9 × 10. Now apply BODMAS (division and multiplication before addition and subtraction): 102.
  10. d — Walk from S back to W, one statement at a time: S’s son is R → R’s mother is W. Combined, that is wife (the path reduces to ‘spouse’, and W is female).
  11. d — I gives x = 10 − 4 = 6. II gives x = 6 or x = −6, which is not one value. So I alone is sufficient.
  12. d — The machines that are toys may all be non-engines, so I does not follow. ‘Some machines are toys’ turns round to ‘Some toys are machines’, so II follows. (Checked against every model of the premises: I does not, II follows.)
  13. c — Two ‘all’ statements chain: doctors ⊂ graduates ⊂ readers, so I follows. ‘All doctors are readers’ also gives ‘Some readers are doctors’ by conversion, so II follows. (Checked against every model of the premises: I follows, II follows.)
  14. a — Applying the clues leaves exactly one arrangement (left to right: E A D B C). The extreme left is E. (Checked by trying all 120 arrangements: exactly one satisfies every clue.)
  15. a — The only arrangement that satisfies every clue is E A D B C, so the person second from the right is B.

Section 2 — Technical Ability (mathematical)

  1. b — Students in the first 3 groups: 24 + 94 + 105 = 223. Percentage = 223/447 × 100 = 49.89%.
  2. d — Convert each share to marks (400 × share): Hindi 140, Science 80, Maths 80, English 60, History 40. Only History is below 60. Best − worst = 140 − 40 = 100.
  3. c — Total = 290 + 660 + 660 + 860 + 260 = 2730; average = 2730/5 = 546.
  4. d — Eliminate one variable (multiply and subtract): x = -19, y = -32. So x > y.
  5. a — Solve the pair: a pen costs ₹12, a pencil ₹3. Then 3×12 + 5×3 = ₹51.
  6. a — (4/3)πr³ = 130977 → r³ = 31255.88 → r = 31.5. Surface area = 4πr² = 4(22/7)(31.5)² = 12474 cm².
  7. a — Net per hour = 1/15 − 1/30 = 1/30. Time = 30 hours.
  8. c — 3! = 6, divided by the factorials of the repeats (E ×2): 6 ÷ 2 = 3.
  9. a — Reduce the base first: 3 ≡ 3 (mod 5). Repeated multiplication mod 5 gives remainder 2.
  10. c — Marked price = 1000 × 120/100 = 1200. Selling price = marked × 90/100 = 1080. Profit = (1080 − 1000) / 1000 × 100 = 8%. (Not 20 − 10 = 10%: the discount applies to the marked price.)

Section 3 — Verbal Ability

  1. d — The passage says so directly: ‘plants absorb heat that would otherwise warm the roof’.
  2. d — The roof's strength, carrying water and pests are all named; seed prices are not mentioned.
  3. a — ‘Self-reliant’ = relying on oneself.
  4. d — The passage lists benefits, names real problems and reports supporters' limited claim (‘even if … cannot feed a whole city’): a cautiously positive view.
  5. b — The passage states: ‘hoping that more people will take up the idea’.
  6. b — Neither takes a singular verb: ‘was able to answer’.
  7. c — Since needs a point in time; a length of time takes for: ‘for five years’.
  8. d — Furniture is uncountable and singular: ‘is made of’.
  9. c — Each is singular, ‘must submit’ is correct, and ‘his or her’ agrees with it. There is no error.
  10. b — A bonus is a reward, so the feeling is positive: impressed.
  11. b — Despite signals contrast with the rain, and the past tense fits: went ahead.
  12. b — Two people: the comparative with the — the taller of the two.
  13. a — Correct order: PRQS. Their leaves needs forests; This exchange = absorbing and releasing; That is why concludes.
  14. c — Correct order: QSRP. It = the budget; the two = earning and spending; Those savings = the money saved by cutting waste.
  15. b — Meticulous means very careful about details: painstaking.
  16. c — Abundant = existing in large quantities: plentiful.
  17. d — Transparent lets light through; its opposite is opaque.
  18. c — It means studying or working far into the night.
  19. d — Without the size and selection of the sample, ‘9 out of 10’ tells us little about everyone.
  20. c — ‘Whenever’ is a generalisation; one change in conditions (cleared drains) can break it, so the conclusion is not certain.

Section 4 — Pseudocode

  1. a — Trace it, recording the variables at the end of each pass:
pass i n s

2 2 13 2
3 3 13 2
4 4 13 2
5 5 13 12
6 6 13 12
7 7 13 12

(… 6 more passes)

The program prints 56. Output: 56. 47. a — Trace it, recording the variables at the end of each pass:

pass c i j
1 4 1 7
2 7 2 7
3 10 3 7

The program prints 10. Output: 10. 48. b — i starts at 7. Evaluate left to right: --i makes i 6 then reads 6; i++ reads 6 then i becomes 7 → result = 0, and i ends at 7. 49. b — Each call doubles x and lowers y: f(3,4) → f(6,3) → f(12,2) → f(24,1) → f(48,0) = 48. 50. d — 12 = 1100 and 10 = 1010 in binary. AND = 1000 = 8; OR = 1110 = 14; XOR = 0110 = 6. Sum = 8 + 14 + 6 = 28.

Section 5 — Numerical Puzzle

  1. b — Only the repeated number matters: the third number is n² + 1. n=4 → 17; n=9 → 82; n=11 → 122. For n = 12: 145.
  2. a — The rule is first = second × third: 36 from 6 and 6; 16 from 8 and 2; 27 from 9 and 3. Last row: 48.
  3. a — The rule that fits all three figures is centre = 2(a+b+c+d). [7, 3, 10, 13] → 66; [6, 9, 10, 2] → 54; [3, 15, 8, 3] → 58. For [10, 9, 8, 12] it gives 78.
  4. d — Test rules on the first line, then confirm on the second. The rule that fits all three lines is digit-sum(a + b): (78, 45) → 6; (11, 61) → 9; (54, 13) → 13. Applied to (21, 74) it gives 14. (5 candidate rules were tried; only this one fits.)

Section 6 — English Grammar

  1. a — Thousands of students is plural and the sentence is general: apply.
  2. b — The phrase is begins with.
  3. c — Students who practise … feel: plural subject, general present.
  4. a — Learn to manage: the infinitive.
  5. a — The sentence adds a last point in the list; Finally fits, the others break the grammar.

Section 7 — English Writing

There is no key. Mark yourself with this checklist (1 point each, out of 6): on topic · clear introduction · three developed points · a conclusion · grammar and spelling checked · about 190 words. Four or more is a safe pass. Compare your structure with topics/W1-english-writing.md.
