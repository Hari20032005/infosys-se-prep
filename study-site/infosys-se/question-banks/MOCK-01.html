MOCK-01 — Full-length Systems Engineer mock test

Structure (the observed Systems Engineer pattern, research/03-pattern-synthesis.md §1): 7 sections, 60 questions, 120 minutes, 75 marks plus an essay. No negative marking has been reported, so answer every question.

# Section Questions Marks Minutes
1 Reasoning Ability 15 15 25
2 Technical Ability (mathematical) 10 10 35
3 Verbal Ability 20 20 20
4 Pseudocode 5 10 10
5 Numerical Puzzle 4 10 (2 + 2 + 3 + 3) 10
6 English Grammar 5 10 10
7 English Writing 1 unpublished 10

How to sit it. One sitting, phone away, no calculator, rough paper only. Set a timer for each section and stop when it ends, even mid-question: the real sections are timed and cannot be revisited [LIKELY]. Do not look at MOCK-01-ANSWERS.md until the essay is finished.

Pace. Reasoning ≈ 100 s a question · Technical ≈ 3.5 min · Verbal ≈ 60 s · Pseudocode ≈ 2 min · Puzzle ≈ 2.5 min · Grammar ≈ 2 min.

Section 1 — Reasoning Ability

15 questions · 15 marks · 25 minutes

1. [Position rule] In a certain code SILVER is written as 19012050. How is JUNGLE written in that code? a) 1021147125 b) 100140120 c) 0210410012 d) 0210705

2. [Position rule] In a certain code SMART is written as 0130180. How is FLOWER written in that code? a) 6121523518 b) 6015050 c) 012023010 d) 012023018

3. [Average of positions] In a certain code LAST is written as 13 and NORTH as 15. What is the code for ENGINE? a) 54 b) 9 c) 10 d) 8

4. [Prime-based] Find the next term: 13, 17, 19, 23, 29, ? a) 33 b) 37 c) 31 d) 32

5. [Prime-based] Find the next term: 17, 19, 23, 29, 31, ? a) 39 b) 37 c) 33 d) 38

6. [Number-letter-number] Find the next term: 8V6, 15T10, 24R14, 35P18, 48N22, ? a) 65L26 b) 63N26 c) 63L26 d) 63L24

7. [Two-term recurrence] Find the next term: 0, 2, 2, 4, 6, 10, 16, ? a) 28 b) 52 c) 26 d) 32

8. [Divide then digit-sum] If 536 ★ 8 = 13 and 198 ★ 2 = 18, find 630 ★ 6. a) 12 b) 6 c) 105 d) 7

9. [Sign substitution] If ‘+’ means ‘×’, ‘−’ means ‘+’, ‘×’ means ‘−’, what is the value of 5 − 7 − 9 + 10 ? a) -1 b) -102 c) 102 d) 104

10. [Relation chain] W is the mother of R. R is the son of S. How is W related to S? a) sister-in-law b) daughter-in-law c) niece d) wife

11. [Data sufficiency] Is x a single number? What is the value of x?

I. x + y = 10 and y = 4. II. x² = 36. a) Both statements together are needed b) Statement II alone is sufficient c) Neither statement is sufficient d) Statement I alone is sufficient

12. [Syllogism] Statements: All engines are machines. Some machines are toys. Conclusions:

I. Some engines are toys. II. Some toys are machines. a) Neither conclusion follows b) Only conclusion I follows c) Both conclusions follow d) Only conclusion II follows

13. [Syllogism] Statements: All doctors are graduates. All graduates are readers. Conclusions:

I. All doctors are readers. II. Some readers are doctors. a) Neither conclusion follows b) Only conclusion II follows c) Both conclusions follow d) Only conclusion I follows

14. [Seating] Five friends A, B, C, D and E sit in a row facing north. B and C sit next to each other. E does not sit next to D. D sits exactly in the middle. D sits immediately to the left of B.

Who sits at the extreme left end of the row? a) E b) C c) B d) A

15. [Seating] Five friends A, B, C, D and E sit in a row facing north. B and C sit next to each other. E does not sit next to D. D sits exactly in the middle. D sits immediately to the left of B.

Who sits second from the right end? a) B b) D c) C d) A

Section 2 — Technical Ability (mathematical)

10 questions · 10 marks · 35 minutes

16. [Frequency table] The heights of 447 students are grouped as shown.

Height (cm) Students
140–150 24
150–160 94
160–170 105
170–180 120
180–190 104

What percentage of the students are shorter than 170 cm? a) 50.11% b) 49.89% c) 65.01% d) 23.49%

17. [Pie chart as a table] A student scored 400 marks in total. The share of the total earned in each subject is shown. The pass mark in each subject is 60.

Subject Share of total marks
Hindi 35%
Science 20%
Maths 20%
English 15%
History 10%

In which subject did the student fail, and what is the difference between the best and the worst subject scores? a) History, 112 b) English, 100 c) History, 88 d) History, 100

18. [Average] Study the table.

Year Plant A (units) Plant B (units)
2019 290 350
2020 660 590
2021 660 230
2022 860 440
2023 260 210

What was Plant A's average yearly output over the five years? a) 364 b) 2730 c) 546 d) 610

19. [Two equations] 1x + 4y = -147 and 6x + 5y = -274. Which relation holds between x and y? a) x = y b) cannot be determined c) x < y d) x > y

20. [Cost of items] 4 pens and 1 pencils cost ₹51. 5 pens and 3 pencils cost ₹69. What is the cost of 3 pens and 5 pencils? a) 51 b) 120 c) 48 d) 63

21. [Sphere] The volume of a sphere is 130977 cm³. Find its surface area (π = 22/7). a) 12474 b) 6237 c) 3118.5 d) 396

22. [Pipe and leak] A tap fills a tank in 15 hours. A leak at the bottom can empty the full tank in 30 hours. If both are open, how many hours does it take to fill the tank? a) 30 b) 32 c) 22.5 d) 10

23. [Arrangements] In how many distinct ways can the letters of the word EEL be arranged? a) 13 b) 10 c) 3 d) 6

24. [Remainder of a power] What is the remainder when 3^19 is divided by 5? a) 2 b) 3 c) 4 d) 1

25. [Profit and discount] A shopkeeper marks an article 20% above its cost price of ₹1000 and then allows a discount of 10% on the marked price. What is his profit percentage? a) 3% b) 10% c) 8% d) 13%

Section 3 — Verbal Ability

20 questions · 20 marks · 20 minutes

Passage. Read the passage and answer the five questions.

In many crowded cities, open land is scarce and expensive. Some residents have responded by turning rooftops into small farms. Using containers filled with soil or simple water-based systems, they grow vegetables such as tomatoes, spinach and chillies. The practice lowers the household's food bills and gives families fresher produce. It also keeps buildings cooler, because plants absorb heat that would otherwise warm the roof. However, rooftop farming is not without problems. A roof must be strong enough to carry the weight of wet soil, and water must be carried up several floors. Pests can also spread between nearby roofs. City governments in several countries now offer training and small grants, hoping that more people will take up the idea. Supporters argue that, even if rooftop farms cannot feed a whole city, they make urban life greener and more self-reliant.

26. [Reading comprehension] According to the passage, rooftop farms help keep buildings cooler because a) they reflect sunlight with white paint. b) the soil is always wet. c) they block the rain from reaching the roof. d) plants absorb heat that would otherwise warm the roof.

27. [Reading comprehension] Which of the following is not mentioned as a problem of rooftop farming? a) The weight of wet soil on the roof. b) Carrying water up several floors. c) Pests spreading between roofs. d) The high price of seeds.

28. [Reading comprehension] The word ‘self-reliant’ in the last sentence most nearly means a) able to depend on oneself. b) dependent on others. c) extremely wealthy. d) growing quickly.

29. [Reading comprehension] The author's attitude towards rooftop farming is best described as a) completely indifferent. b) wildly enthusiastic. c) strongly opposed. d) cautiously positive.

30. [Reading comprehension] Why do city governments offer training and small grants? a) To reduce the weight on roofs. b) To encourage more people to take up rooftop farming. c) To stop people using their roofs. d) To raise the price of vegetables.

31. [Spotting errors] Find the part of the sentence that has an error.

Neither of the two candidates (a) / were able to answer (b) / all the questions correctly. (c) / No error (d) a) (c) b) (b) c) (a) d) (d)

32. [Spotting errors] Find the part of the sentence that has an error.

She has been working (a) / in this company (b) / since five years. (c) / No error (d) a) (d) b) (b) c) (c) d) (a)

33. [Spotting errors] Find the part of the sentence that has an error.

The furniture in the new office (a) / are made of (b) / teak wood. (c) / No error (d) a) (d) b) (c) c) (a) d) (b)

34. [Spotting errors] Find the part of the sentence that has an error.

Each of the students (a) / must submit (b) / his or her assignment by Friday. (c) / No error (d) a) (a) b) (c) c) (d) d) (b)

35. [Sentence completion] The manager was so ___ with the quality of the work that he gave the team a bonus. a) anxious b) impressed c) disappointed d) unaware

36. [Sentence completion] Despite the heavy rain, the match ___ as planned. a) gets cancelled b) went ahead c) was cancelled d) has gone ahead

37. [Sentence completion] She is ___ of the two sisters. a) the tallest b) the taller c) taller d) tallest

38. [Para-jumble] Rearrange the four sentences into a meaningful paragraph. Choose the correct order.

P. Forests act as the lungs of the planet. Q. This exchange helps keep the air clean and the climate stable. R. Their leaves absorb carbon dioxide and release oxygen. S. That is why cutting them down at a large scale is so dangerous.

a) PRQS b) RQSP c) PRSQ d) RPQS

39. [Para-jumble] Rearrange the four sentences into a meaningful paragraph. Choose the correct order.

P. Those savings can then be put towards a goal. Q. A budget is the first step towards financial stability. R. Comparing the two shows where money is being wasted. S. It lists what you earn and what you spend each month.

a) SRPQ b) QSPR c) QSRP d) SQRP

40. [Synonym] Choose the word closest in meaning to meticulous. a) generous b) painstaking c) hasty d) careless

41. [Synonym] Choose the word closest in meaning to abundant. a) rare b) scarce c) plentiful d) fragile

42. [Antonym] Choose the word opposite in meaning to transparent. a) clear b) bright c) thin d) opaque

43. [Idiom] Choose the meaning of the idiom ‘to burn the midnight oil’. a) to waste fuel b) to be very angry c) to work late into the night d) to start a fire

44. [Flaw] A shampoo advertisement says: ‘9 out of 10 users saw shinier hair after two weeks.’ A viewer concludes that the shampoo works for almost everyone.

The viewer overlooks that a) the advertisement is on television. b) two weeks is a short period. c) shampoo is sold in bottles. d) the advertisement does not say how many users were tested or how they were chosen.

45. [Weaken] Mohan says: ‘Whenever it rains, the road near my house floods. It rained today, so the road near my house must be flooded.’

Which of the following correctly states a weakness in the reasoning? a) Rain is seasonal. b) Mohan lives near a road. c) It is possible the drains were cleared today, so the usual flooding did not occur. d) Floods only happen at night.

Section 4 — Pseudocode

5 questions · 10 marks · 10 minutes

46. [Trace] What is the output of the following pseudocode? (All variables are integers; / is integer division; MOD is the remainder.)

1) Integer i, n, s
2) Set s = 0
3) Set n = 13
4) For (each i from 1 to n)
5)   If (i MOD 4 == 1) then
6)     s = s + i * 2
7)   End-if
8) End-for
9) Print s

a) 56 b) 57 c) 58 d) 55

47. [Trace] What is the output of the following pseudocode? (All variables are integers; / is integer division; MOD is the remainder.)

1) Integer c, i, j
2) Set c = 0
3) Set j = 0
4) For (each i from 1 to 3)
5)   For (each j from i to 7)
6)     If ((i + j) MOD 2 == 0) then
7)       c++
8)     End-if
9)   End-for
10) End-for
11) Print c

a) 10 b) 8 c) 11 d) 9

48. [Increment operators] What is the value of result after this code runs? Java-style left-to-right evaluation.

int i = 7;
int result = --i - i++;
print(result, i);

a) -1 b) 0 c) 1 d) 2

49. [Recursion] What is the output of the following pseudocode?

Function f(x, y)
    if (y == 0)
        return x
    return f(x * 2, y - 1)

Print f(3, 4)

a) 96 b) 48 c) 11 d) 24

50. [Bitwise] What is the output of the following pseudocode? (& is bitwise AND, | OR, ^ XOR.)

Set integer a = 12
Set integer b = 10
Print (a & b) + (a | b) + (a ^ b)

a) 24 b) 20 c) 22 d) 28

  36  6  6
  16  8  2
  27  9  3
   ?  8  6

a) 48 b) 49 c) 60 d) 58

53. [Figure rule] Each figure has four outer numbers in the order (a, b, c, d) and one centre number. Find the missing centre number:

outer [7, 3, 10, 13] → centre 66
outer [6, 9, 10, 2] → centre 54
outer [3, 15, 8, 3] → centre 58
outer [10, 9, 8, 12] → centre ?

a) 78 b) 80 c) 79 d) 77

(78, 45) → 6
(11, 61) → 9
(54, 13) → 13
(21, 74) → ?

a) 16 b) 15 c) 12 d) 14
