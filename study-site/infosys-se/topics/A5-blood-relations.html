A5 — Blood relations

Tier 2 · Reasoning Ability · 1.5% of marks · about 0.5 h · confidence Medium

1. Why this topic matters here

Expect about 1.2 questions and 1.2 marks on this topic (about 1.5% of the 75 scored marks). It sits in Reasoning Ability, part of the 25-question aptitude block (Reasoning Ability 15 Q / 25 min + Technical Ability 10 Q / 35 min, one cut-off each). Tier 2, 0.5 hours to reach reliable recall, confidence Medium.

1 question on this topic are in the observed Infosys pool (R13), from 1 source. They are listed in §2 and solved in ../question-banks/QB-99-OBSERVED-POOL.md.

Why the tier: expected marks per study hour, weighted by confidence, is 1.85 against a paper average of 1.84 (../data/analysis-tables.md §6). Every section has its own cut-off, so a zero here costs the whole section's chance, not only these marks. There is no calculator. Negative marking: none reported.

2. Exactly what gets asked

One observed item, a coded relation question, plus the classic chain questions every Indian aptitude paper uses [REPORTED]. Blood relations is low-effort: the method below takes about half an hour to learn.

Sub-pattern How it appears Observed
Symbol-coded relations "X + Y means X is the sister of Y, X − Y means X is the father of Y, X × Y means X is the brother of Y. Which means M is the son of S?" R13 (reported twice in the same channel)
Chain of statements "P is the son of R. R is the father of Q. How is P related to Q?" authored from the pattern
Pointing at a photo "Pointing to a man, a woman said, ‘His mother is the only daughter of my mother.’" authored, classic

3. Concept notes

3.1 Draw it

Draw each person as a dot with M or F. Draw vertical lines for parent → child, horizontal lines for siblings, and a double line for a married couple. Every blood-relation question is then a path between two dots.

3.2 Three moves

Move Meaning
Up to a parent
Down to a child
Side to a sibling

A path is a sequence of moves; its name depends on the sequence and on the sex of the destination.

Path Name
Up father / mother
Down son / daughter
Side brother / sister
Up, Up grandfather / grandmother
Down, Down grandson / granddaughter
Up, Side uncle / aunt
Side, Down nephew / niece
Up, Side, Down cousin
Spouse, Up father-in-law / mother-in-law
Down, Spouse son-in-law / daughter-in-law
Spouse, Side or Side, Spouse brother-in-law / sister-in-law

Two shortcuts: Up then Down is Side (siblings share a parent), and Side then Up is Up (your sibling's parent is your parent).

3.3 Symbol-coded relations (R13)

Translate each symbol into words before anything else. R13: S − P means S is P's father; P + M means P is M's sister; M × G means M is G's brother. So S is the father of P and M is P's sibling, so S is M's father as well; and M is male (a brother). Therefore M is the son of S, and the expression S − P + M × G is correct.

3.4 "Only"

"The only daughter of my mother" is me if I am female. "My mother's only son" is me if I am male. "Only" collapses the family down to one person.

3.5 Gender rules

The word “brother”, “father”, “son”, “husband” fixes the speaker as male; “sister”, “mother”, “daughter”, “wife” fixes female. A name never fixes gender: use letters and the words only.

4. Must-memorise block

Item Answer
Mother's brother maternal uncle
Father's sister paternal aunt
Uncle's or aunt's child cousin
Sister's husband brother-in-law
Wife's brother brother-in-law
Son's wife daughter-in-law
Husband's mother mother-in-law
Brother's son nephew
Brother's daughter niece
Father's father paternal grandfather

5. Worked examples

Example 1 — coded relation (R13). Above: S − P + M × G.

Example 2 — chain. “U is the son of R. R is the father of Q. How is U related to Q?” R is the parent of both U and Q, so U and Q are siblings. U is male (son), so U is Q's brother.

Example 3 — chain with an in-law. “J is the sister of I. I is the wife of C. How is J related to C?” From C: Spouse (I), then Side (J) → sister-in-law. J is female. Sister-in-law.

Example 4 — the photo. The woman says: “His mother is the only daughter of my mother.” My mother's only daughter is me. So his mother is me: the woman is the man's mother.

Example 5 — three generations. “A is B's sister. C is B's mother. D is C's father. How is A related to D?” D is C's father; C is A's mother; so D is A's grandfather, and A is D's granddaughter.

6. Traps and gotchas

Trap Why it catches people How to avoid it
Assuming gender from a name Names are not fixed in the question Take gender only from the relation word
Mixing maternal and paternal The question may not ask which Answer at the level the options use
Forgetting that “brother” implies a male M × G in R13 fixes M as male Mark M/F on your diagram
Answering from the wrong person “How is A related to D” vs “D to A” Write the direction in the margin
Treating “only” loosely It collapses to the speaker Replace “the only daughter of my mother” with “me” if female
Reversing the direction of a coded symbol + could mean sister of Y or Y of sister Write the meaning in words first

7. Practice questions

More questions: QB-A5-blood-relations.md.

1. [Relation chain] P is the mother of V. V is the son of Q. How is P related to Q? a) granddaughter b) daughter c) wife d) sister-in-law

2. [Relation chain] T is the daughter of U. U is the father of S. S is the mother of P. How is T related to P? a) niece b) aunt c) granddaughter d) sister

3. [Relation chain] U is the mother of T. T is the father of S. How is U related to S? a) granddaughter b) daughter c) grandmother d) mother-in-law

4. [Relation chain] U is the son of V. V is the daughter of W. How is U related to W? a) husband b) brother c) uncle d) grandson

5. [Relation chain] S is the brother of R. R is the brother of W. How is S related to W? a) father b) father-in-law c) brother d) grandfather

6. [Relation chain] S is the daughter of W. W is the husband of R. How is S related to R? a) sister-in-law b) wife c) mother-in-law d) daughter

7. [Relation chain] P is the son of S. S is the wife of T. How is P related to T? a) brother b) son c) uncle d) brother-in-law

8. [Relation chain] S is the son of U. U is the husband of W. W is the daughter of V. How is S related to V? a) grandson b) father c) brother-in-law d) husband

9. [Relation chain] P is the mother of U. U is the mother of W. How is P related to W? a) grandmother b) wife c) granddaughter d) aunt

10. [Relation chain] U is the son of Q. Q is the son of P. How is U related to P? a) son b) grandson c) brother d) nephew

11. [Relation chain] Q is the daughter of U. U is the son of P. P is the mother of S. How is Q related to S? a) niece b) mother c) granddaughter d) wife

12. [Relation chain] R is the sister of V. V is the daughter of S. S is the father of T. How is R related to T? a) daughter-in-law b) sister-in-law c) daughter d) sister

ANSWER KEY

  1. c — Walk from Q back to P, one statement at a time: Q’s son is V → V’s mother is P. Combined, that is wife (the path reduces to ‘spouse’, and P is female).
  2. b — Walk from P back to T, one statement at a time: P’s mother is S → S’s father is U → U’s daughter is T. Combined, that is aunt (the path reduces to ‘up then side’, and T is female).
  3. c — Walk from S back to U, one statement at a time: S’s father is T → T’s mother is U. Combined, that is grandmother (the path reduces to ‘up then up’, and U is female).
  4. d — Walk from W back to U, one statement at a time: W’s daughter is V → V’s son is U. Combined, that is grandson (the path reduces to ‘down then down’, and U is male).
  5. c — Walk from W back to S, one statement at a time: W’s brother is R → R’s brother is S. Combined, that is brother (the path reduces to ‘side’, and S is male).
  6. d — Walk from R back to S, one statement at a time: R’s husband is W → W’s daughter is S. Combined, that is daughter (the path reduces to ‘down’, and S is female).
  7. b — Walk from T back to P, one statement at a time: T’s wife is S → S’s son is P. Combined, that is son (the path reduces to ‘down’, and P is male).
  8. a — Walk from V back to S, one statement at a time: V’s daughter is W → W’s husband is U → U’s son is S. Combined, that is grandson (the path reduces to ‘down then down’, and S is male).
  9. a — Walk from W back to P, one statement at a time: W’s mother is U → U’s mother is P. Combined, that is grandmother (the path reduces to ‘up then up’, and P is female).
  10. b — Walk from P back to U, one statement at a time: P’s son is Q → Q’s son is U. Combined, that is grandson (the path reduces to ‘down then down’, and U is male).
  11. a — Walk from S back to Q, one statement at a time: S’s mother is P → P’s son is U → U’s daughter is Q. Combined, that is niece (the path reduces to ‘side then down’, and Q is female).
  12. d — Walk from T back to R, one statement at a time: T’s father is S → S’s daughter is V → V’s sister is R. Combined, that is sister (the path reduces to ‘side’, and R is female).
