A16 — Time and work

Tier 4 · Technical Ability · 0.7% of marks · about 0.8 h · confidence Low

1. Why this topic matters here

Expect about 0.5 questions and 0.5 marks on this topic (about 0.7% of the 75 scored marks). It sits in Technical Ability, part of the 25-question aptitude block (Reasoning Ability 15 Q / 25 min + Technical Ability 10 Q / 35 min, one cut-off each). Tier 4, 0.8 hours to reach reliable recall, confidence Low.

No Infosys question on this topic was recovered. It rests on syllabus lists or first-hand naming only (C1), so its share is an allocation, not an observation.

Why the tier: expected marks per study hour, weighted by confidence, is 0.39 against a paper average of 1.84 (../data/analysis-tables.md §6). Every section has its own cut-off, so a zero here costs the whole section's chance, not only these marks. There is no calculator. Negative marking: none reported.

2. Exactly what gets asked

Tier 4, recognise-only. Time and work is on the 2025 deck's Technical Ability list (C1) [REPORTED]; no observed item. Four forms cover it.

Sub-pattern How it appears
Two workers together A in 12 days, B in 15 days; together?
One leaves or joins midway B works 4 days, A finishes
Pipes and a leak A tap fills a tank in 10 h; a leak empties it in 15 h
Men and days 12 men take 8 days; 16 men take?

3. Concept notes

  • One day's work = 1 / days. A in 12 days does 1/12 per day.
  • Together = add the rates. 1/12 + 1/15 = 9/60 = 3/20 → 20/3 days.
  • Pipes: inlet adds, outlet (leak) subtracts.
  • Men × days = constant work (man-days). 12 × 8 = 96 man-days; with 16 men: 96 / 16 = 6 days.
  • Shortcut for two people: days together = ab / (a + b).

4. Must-memorise block

Item Formula
Together ab / (a + b)
Fill and leak ab / (b − a) where b > a
Work rate × time
Man-days men × days = constant

5. Worked examples

Example 1. A 12 days, B 15 days: 12 × 15 / 27 = 180/27 = 6⅔ days.

Example 2. A tap fills in 10 h, a leak empties in 15 h: 10 × 15 / 5 = 30 hours.

Example 3. B works 5 days of a 20-day job, A (15 days alone) finishes: left 3/4; A needs 3/4 × 15 = 11.25 days.

Example 4. 12 men, 8 days → 96 man-days; 16 men → 6 days.

6. Traps and gotchas

Trap Why it catches people How to avoid it
Adding days instead of rates 12 + 15 Always convert to per-day work
Forgetting that a leak subtracts Treating it as a second tap Sign: −
Mixing man-days and days 12 men × 8 days is 96 man-days Keep units
Partial work Fractions of the job Subtract done work from 1

7. Practice questions

More questions: QB-A16-time-and-work.md.

1. [Two workers] A can finish a job in 30 days and B can finish it in 45 days. Working together, in how many days will they finish it? a) 19 b) 37.5 c) 18 d) 90

2. [Pipe and leak] A tap fills a tank in 20 hours. A leak at the bottom can empty the full tank in 30 hours. If both are open, how many hours does it take to fill the tank? a) 62 b) 12 c) 25 d) 60

3. [Partly done job] B works alone for 4 days on a job he could finish in 12 days. A then completes the rest, and A alone needs 60 days for the whole job. How many days does A work? a) 40 b) 56 c) 10 d) 44

4. [Pipe and leak] A tap fills a tank in 6 hours. A leak at the bottom can empty the full tank in 30 hours. If both are open, how many hours does it take to fill the tank? a) 7.5 b) 5 c) 18 d) 9.5

5. [Partly done job] B works alone for 2 days on a job he could finish in 60 days. A then completes the rest, and A alone needs 15 days for the whole job. How many days does A work? a) 16.5 b) 13 c) 14.5 d) 12

6. [Partly done job] B works alone for 5 days on a job he could finish in 12 days. A then completes the rest, and A alone needs 40 days for the whole job. How many days does A work? a) 28.33 b) 23.33 c) 35 d) 9.23

7. [Men and days] 12 men can build a wall in 5 days. How many days will 4 men take to build the same wall (all work at the same rate)? a) 23 b) 17 c) 24 d) 15

8. [Two workers] A can finish a job in 15 days and B can finish it in 12 days. Working together, in how many days will they finish it? a) 7.67 b) 6.67 c) 13.5 d) 60

ANSWER KEY

  1. c — Per day A does 1/30 and B 1/45; together 1/18 of the job. Days = 1 ÷ that = 18.
  2. d — Net per hour = 1/20 − 1/30 = 1/60. Time = 60 hours.
  3. a — B does 4/12 = 1/3. Left: 2/3. A does 1/60 per day, so 2/3 × 60 = 40 days.
  4. a — Net per hour = 1/6 − 1/30 = 2/15. Time = 7.5 hours.
  5. c — B does 2/60 = 1/30. Left: 29/30. A does 1/15 per day, so 29/30 × 15 = 14.5 days.
  6. b — B does 5/12 = 5/12. Left: 7/12. A does 1/40 per day, so 7/12 × 40 = 23.33 days.
  7. d — Total work = 12 × 5 = 60 man-days. With 4 men: 60/4 = 15 days.
  8. b — Per day A does 1/15 and B 1/12; together 3/20 of the job. Days = 1 ÷ that = 6.67.
