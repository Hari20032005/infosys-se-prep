A7 — Data interpretation: tables and charts

Tier 1 · Technical Ability · 3.1% of marks · about 1.0 h · confidence High

1. Why this topic matters here

Expect about 2.3 questions and 2.3 marks on this topic (about 3.1% of the 75 scored marks). It sits in Technical Ability, part of the 25-question aptitude block (Reasoning Ability 15 Q / 25 min + Technical Ability 10 Q / 35 min, one cut-off each). Tier 1, 1.0 hours to reach reliable recall, confidence High.

3 questions on this topic are in the observed Infosys pool (Q03, Q07), from 2 sources. They are listed in §2 and solved in ../question-banks/QB-99-OBSERVED-POOL.md.

Why the tier: expected marks per study hour, weighted by confidence, is 2.31 against a paper average of 1.84 (../data/analysis-tables.md §6). Every section has its own cut-off, so a zero here costs the whole section's chance, not only these marks. There is no calculator. Negative marking: none reported.

2. Exactly what gets asked

Three coaches independently say data interpretation is Infosys's favourite: "there is no exam of Infosys where data interpretation questions is not asked" (YT-2YHL) and "this year more DI questions are getting asked" (YT-2NTB) [REPORTED]. Data is shown as a table or as percentages that you turn into a table, then one or two computations follow.

Sub-pattern How it appears Observed
Frequency table, "percentage less than a value" 400 students in height groups, find the percentage shorter than 170 cm Q03
Percentage shares of a total, pass/fail and gap Marks as % of 400, pass mark 50, find the failed subject and the best–worst difference Q07
Histogram with a pass mark Numbers of students in score ranges, what proportion passed discarded (X02: chart values not recoverable)
Bar data, growth or ratio Output of two plants over five years authored from the pattern

3. Concept notes

3.1 Read the chart before you read the question

Write down: what the numbers are (units, thousands), what the total is, and what each row is a percentage of. Most DI errors are a wrong base, not wrong arithmetic.

3.2 The five calculations that cover almost everything

Question Formula
What percentage is A of B A / B × 100
Percentage change from old to new (new − old) / old × 100
Ratio of A to B A : B, divide both by the HCF
Average total ÷ count
Share of the total part ÷ total × 100

3.3 Percentage charts become tables

If a pie chart says "History 13%" and the total is 400 marks, History = 13% of 400 = 52. Do this for every slice before reading the question; every answer then comes from the table.

3.4 Frequency tables

"How many are shorter than 170 cm" means the groups that end at or below 170, added up. Percentage of the class = that sum ÷ total × 100.

3.5 Approximation

With no calculator, round to round numbers and compare with the options. If options are far apart (77%, 67%, 57%), a rough figure is enough.

Always the old value is the base for a change. Percentage points differ from percentage change.

5. Worked examples

Example 1 — frequency table (observed Q03). 400 students: 140–150 cm: 150; 150–160: 88; 160–170: 70; 170–180: 50; 180–190: 42. Shorter than 170 cm = first three groups = 150 + 88 + 70 = 308. 308 / 400 = 77%. Sanity check: 150 + 88 + 70 + 50 + 42 = 400 ✓.

Example 2 — shares of a total (observed Q07). Total 400. English 25%, Hindi 34%, Maths 18%, Science 10%, History 13%. Marks: English 100, Hindi 136, Maths 72, Science 40, History 52. Pass mark 50 → fails in Science only. Best − worst = 136 − 40 = 96. (The recalled stem says "out of 100", which Hindi's 136 contradicts; the answer holds on the chart data.)

Example 3 — quarterly sales. Sales in thousands: Q1 120, Q2 150, Q3 135, Q4 180. Total = 585. - Growth Q1 → Q2: (150 − 120) / 120 × 100 = 25%. - Average = 585 / 4 = 146.25. - Share of Q4 in the year = 180 / 585 × 100 = 30.77% (4/13). - Ratio Q4 : Q1 = 180 : 120 = 3 : 2.

Example 4 — a fall, not a rise. Output went from 730 to 560. Change = (560 − 730) / 730 = −170/730 ≈ −23.3%. The base is 730, not 560.

6. Traps and gotchas

Trap Why it catches people How to avoid it
Wrong base for percentage change Dividing by the new value Always divide by the old value
Percentage of the wrong total Charts have a total and a subtotal Write "100% = …" before you start
Adding the wrong groups "Shorter than 170" means up to 170 Mark the cut-off group before adding
Mixing units Thousands against units Write the unit next to the table
Percentage points vs percent 8% → 10% is +2 points but +25% Read the question for which one is asked
Doing arithmetic with options far apart Wastes time Estimate and pick the closest option

Year Plant A (units) Plant B (units)
2019 370 400
2020 610 330
2021 390 370
2022 350 790
2023 410 730

What was Plant A's average yearly output over the five years? a) 2130 b) 426 c) 440 d) 524

ANSWER KEY

  1. d — 230 : 820, divide both by 10 → 23:82.
  2. c — Total = 260 + 330 + 260 + 570 + 500 = 1920; average = 1920/5 = 384.
  3. c — Convert each share to marks (400 × share): Geography 120, Hindi 100, Science 80, English 60, Maths 40. Only Maths is below 50. Best − worst = 120 − 40 = 80.
  4. b — Students in the first 3 groups: 33 + 21 + 40 = 94. Percentage = 94/242 × 100 = 38.84%.
  5. c — Students in the first 4 groups: 116 + 20 + 68 + 95 = 299. Percentage = 299/325 × 100 = 92%.
  6. d — Students in the first 2 groups: 24 + 75 = 99. Percentage = 99/318 × 100 = 31.13%.
  7. d — 400 : 470, divide both by 10 → 40:47.
  8. a — Students in the first 4 groups: 96 + 39 + 83 + 71 = 289. Percentage = 289/364 × 100 = 79.4%.
  9. a — Students in the first 3 groups: 22 + 113 + 80 = 215. Percentage = 215/386 × 100 = 55.7%.
  10. a — Students in the first 2 groups: 22 + 33 = 55. Percentage = 55/312 × 100 = 17.63%.
  11. b — Students in the first 4 groups: 81 + 66 + 116 + 23 = 286. Percentage = 286/327 × 100 = 87.46%.
  12. b — Total = 370 + 610 + 390 + 350 + 410 = 2130; average = 2130/5 = 426.

  5. Do you estimate first when the options are far apart?
