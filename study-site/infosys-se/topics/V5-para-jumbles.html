V5 — Para-jumbles

Tier 3 · Verbal Ability · 2.3% of marks · about 0.6 h · confidence Low

2. Exactly what gets asked

Para-jumbles (re-ordering 4–5 jumbled sentences) are named by the 2026 coach for the Verbal section (V1) alongside error identification and reading comprehension [REPORTED]. No observed instance was recovered, so this file rests on the syllabus list alone and the tier comes from the section floor rather than from measured frequency. It is cheap: about 40 seconds a question once the method is automatic.

Sub-pattern How it appears
Four sentences labelled P, Q, R, S; choose the order from four options The standard form
A fixed first or last sentence, with the rest jumbled Easier; start from the fixed one

3. Concept notes

3.1 The method: openers, links, closers

  1. Find the opener. It introduces the subject in full (a name, a place, a general statement) and does not begin with this, that, these, such, he, she, they, it, however, but, also.
  2. Find the closer. It sums up or looks to the future (thus, therefore, in the end, as a result) or gives a final example.
  3. Link the middle. Look for: - pronoun → noun: a sentence with it, they, this must come after the one that names the noun; - connectors: however, but, therefore, for example, in addition depend on what came before; - time order: years, first, then, finally; - general → specific: a general statement before its example.
  4. Test the order against the options; two options usually differ only in the first pair, so decide the opener first.

3.2 Mandatory pairs

A definition is followed by its example. A problem is followed by its solution. A question is followed by its answer. A sentence ending in a colon is followed by the list or quotation.

4. Must-memorise block

Signal What it tells you
This, these, such at the start Refers to something already named: not the opener
However, but, yet Contrast with the previous sentence
Therefore, thus, hence Result of the previous sentence
For example, for instance Follows the general claim it illustrates
First … second … finally Fixed order
A year or date Chronological order

5. Worked examples

Example 1. P. It was designed by the engineers of Gustave Eiffel’s company and opened in 1889. Q. The Eiffel Tower is one of the most visited monuments in the world. R. Today it draws millions of visitors every year. S. It was built for the 1889 Paris World Fair. Opener: Q names the subject. Then S (why built) → P (who, when; it = the tower) → R (today, the final). Order QSPR.

Example 2. P. However, the cost of electric vehicles is still high. Q. Electric vehicles are becoming popular in many countries. R. As prices fall, more buyers are expected to switch. S. They produce no exhaust and have lower running costs. Opener: Q. S explains the benefits (They = vehicles). P (However) contrasts with the benefits. R (As prices fall) follows the cost problem. QSPR.

Example 3. (first sentence fixed) 1. Effective leaders listen before they speak. P. That trust encourages people to share their ideas. Q. Listening carefully shows respect for every team member. R. This respect builds trust within the team. Each later sentence points back: This respect (R) needs the respect named in Q, and That trust (P) needs the trust named in R. QRP.

6. Traps and gotchas

Trap Why it catches people How to avoid it
Starting with a sentence that has this or he It refers back The opener must be self-contained
Ignoring connectors However needs something to contrast Put the contrast after what it contrasts
Choosing by the first two sentences only Options share the start Test the whole order
Reordering by topic only Time and cause matter Follow chronology and cause
Not reading the options' openers Often only two candidates Decide the opener, then eliminate

7. Practice questions

More questions: QB-V5-para-jumbles.md.

1. [Para-jumble] Rearrange the four sentences into a meaningful paragraph. Choose the correct order.

P. Then the vapour cools, forms clouds and falls back as rain or snow. Q. The water cycle describes how water moves around the Earth. R. This rain collects in rivers and flows back to the sea, and the cycle begins again. S. First, heat from the sun turns water in oceans and rivers into vapour.

a) QSPR b) SPRQ c) QSRP d) SQPR

2. [Para-jumble] Rearrange the four sentences into a meaningful paragraph. Choose the correct order.

P. It grows faster than almost any other plant, sometimes by a metre in a day. Q. Bamboo is a remarkable plant with many uses. R. This speed means it can be harvested again and again without replanting. S. For that reason it now replaces wood in many industries, from furniture to paper.

a) PRSQ b) QPRS c) QPSR d) PQRS

3. [Para-jumble] Rearrange the four sentences into a meaningful paragraph. Choose the correct order.

P. Such changes have made long queues at bank branches much shorter. Q. Mobile apps now let customers transfer money in seconds. R. Banking has changed greatly over the last decade. S. This convenience means routine work, such as checking a balance, no longer needs a branch visit.

a) QRSP b) QSPR c) RQPS d) RQSP

4. [Para-jumble] Rearrange the four sentences into a meaningful paragraph. Choose the correct order.

P. Sleep is as important to health as food and exercise. Q. Those who regularly get less than this find it harder to concentrate. R. For this reason, doctors advise going to bed at a fixed time every day. S. Adults need seven to nine hours of it each night.

a) SPQR b) PSQR c) SQRP d) PSRQ

5. [Para-jumble] Rearrange the four sentences into a meaningful paragraph. Choose the correct order.

P. As a result, students spend more time in libraries than they did a decade ago. Q. These include e-books, research databases and quiet study spaces. R. Modern libraries offer much more than shelves of books. S. Many now provide a range of digital services as well.

a) RSPQ b) RSQP c) SRQP d) SQPR

6. [Para-jumble] Rearrange the four sentences into a meaningful paragraph. Choose the correct order.

P. They run on batteries and produce no exhaust fumes. Q. Until this changes, many buyers will hesitate to switch. R. Electric cars are becoming more common on Indian roads. S. However, charging points are still few outside the big cities.

a) RPSQ b) RPQS c) PRSQ d) PSQR

7. [Para-jumble] Rearrange the four sentences into a meaningful paragraph. Choose the correct order.

P. This small habit adds up to many books in a year. Q. Those books, in turn, sharpen vocabulary and concentration. R. You do not need much time to follow them: twenty minutes is enough. S. Many successful people say they read every day.

a) SRQP b) RSPQ c) RPQS d) SRPQ

8. [Para-jumble] Rearrange the four sentences into a meaningful paragraph. Choose the correct order.

P. Without it, farmers would see their yields fall sharply. Q. Honey bees play a vital role in growing food. R. As they collect nectar, they carry pollen from flower to flower. S. This process allows many crops to produce fruit and seed.

a) QRPS b) RQSP c) QRSP d) RSPQ

9. [Para-jumble] Rearrange the four sentences into a meaningful paragraph. Choose the correct order.

P. Washing removes the extra starch that makes rice sticky. Q. Next, add twice as much water as rice and bring it to a boil. R. The first step in cooking rice is to wash it. S. Finally, lower the heat and let it simmer until the water is absorbed.

a) RPSQ b) PQSR c) RPQS d) PRQS

10. [Para-jumble] Rearrange the four sentences into a meaningful paragraph. Choose the correct order.

P. Such waste blocks drains and pollutes rivers. Q. Much of it is used only once and thrown away within minutes. R. Reducing this harm starts with carrying a reusable bag. S. Plastic waste has become a serious problem in our cities.

a) SQPR b) QPRS c) SQRP d) QSPR

ANSWER KEY

  1. a — Correct order: QSPR. The first sentence defines the topic. First and Then give the order of steps. This rain needs the rain mentioned just before.
  2. b — Correct order: QPRS. It = bamboo, so the bamboo sentence comes first. This speed needs the growth sentence. For that reason needs the harvesting sentence.
  3. d — Correct order: RQSP. General statement first. This convenience needs the apps sentence. Such changes sums up everything before it.
  4. b — Correct order: PSQR. The topic is defined first. Less than this needs the amount just given. For this reason closes with the advice.
  5. b — Correct order: RSQP. Many now provide needs libraries. These include needs services. As a result is the final outcome.
  6. a — Correct order: RPSQ. They = electric cars. However contrasts with the benefit just named. This = the shortage of charging points.
  7. d — Correct order: SRPQ. Follow them needs successful people. This small habit needs the twenty-minute habit. Those books needs many books.
  8. c — Correct order: QRSP. They = bees. This process = pollination just described. Without it = without that process.
  9. c — Correct order: RPQS. First step opens. Washing explains the first step. Next and Finally fix the order.
  10. a — Correct order: SQPR. Much of it needs plastic waste. Such waste refers to the thrown-away plastic. This harm refers to blocked drains and polluted rivers.
