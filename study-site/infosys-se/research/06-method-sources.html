06 — Method Sources for the Core Course

Date: 2026-10-02. Purpose: the Core Course lessons teach methods and rules. This file lists every page that was opened to check a rule, what it actually says, and what it may be cited for. Standing rule from Hari: open the source, record the publisher and date, confirm it is the kind of page it claims to be, and state only what was observed. A search snippet, a tool's summary, or an HTTP 200 is not enough.

How pages were read. Static pages were downloaded and their text searched directly (curl plus a text extractor, so no model summary stands between me and the page). Pages that need JavaScript or refuse curl were read in the built-in browser or through a fetch tool that returns a summary; those are marked (summary) and used only for the specific sentences the summary quoted.

What is not sourced here, on purpose. Arithmetic, logic and pseudocode answers are not "sourced" from the web at all. They are verified by running code (brute force, exhaustive model search, real Java), which is stronger than citing a page. Web sources are cited only for rules and conventions (Java semantics, English grammar, the vocabulary of argument questions).

S1 Java Language Specification, SE 21, ch. 15 Expressions Oracle · Java SE 21 edition Language specification (primary) §15.7: the language "guarantees that the operands of operators appear to be evaluated in a specific evaluation order, namely, from left to right"; §15.7.1: the left operand is fully evaluated before any part of the right; §15.14.2 and §15.15.1 define postfix and prefix increment; §15.17.2: integer division rounds toward 0; §15.17.3: remainder satisfies (a/b)*b + (a%b) == a and is negative only if the dividend is negative The same section recommends not relying on this order for clarity. That is advice about readable code, not a statement that the result is undefined. A fetch tool's summary of this page said i++ + ++i "depends on implementation details"; the page text says the opposite, so the summary was discarded. Real Java runs in data/verify_generated.py agree with the specification
S2 The Java Tutorials: Assignment, Arithmetic, and Unary Operators · Operators · Bitwise and Bit Shift · Arrays · while / do-while · for · Branching Oracle · © 1995, 2024 Official tutorial Prefix ++result evaluates to the incremented value, postfix result++ to the original value; the precedence table (postfix, then unary, then multiplicative, and so on) and "all binary operators except assignment are evaluated from left to right"; &, ^, | are bitwise AND, XOR, OR; array numbering begins at 0; a do-while body runs at least once; the three parts of a for; break and continue Page says it is written for JDK 8. Used only for rules that have not changed
S3 Making Subjects and Verbs Agree Purdue University Online Writing Lab · ©2026, no page date Reference handout, numbered guidelines (read directly) each, either, neither, everyone, nobody, someone take a singular verb; news and mathematics take a singular verb, scissors and trousers a plural one; doesn't goes with a singular subject, don't with a plural; collective nouns (group, team, family) take a singular verb; ignore a phrase between subject and verb; with there is/are the subject follows the verb; and makes a plural subject, or/nor agree with the nearer subject Has no rule for "the number of" versus "a number of"; that needs another source (gap, §3)
S4 Prepositions of Time, Place, and Introducing Objects Purdue OWL · ©1995-2018 and ©2026, no page date Handout for multilingual writers on with days, at with clock times and noon/night, in with months, years, seasons and parts of the day; since marks a starting point, for a length of time, by a deadline, during within a period, within a maximum —
S5 Articles: A versus An · How to Use Articles (a/an/the) · Count, Noncount Nouns with Articles, Adjectives Purdue OWL · ©1995, no page date Reference handouts an before a vowel sound, a before a consonant sound (the choice follows the sound, not the spelling; silent h takes an); the points to a specific thing, a/an to any one; information, advice, help, knowledge are uncountable (no plural, no a), with the example "The information in your files is correct" —
S6 Active Verb Tenses · Verb Tense Consistency Purdue OWL · ©1995-2018, no page date Reference handouts Present perfect for states that began in the past and include the present ("He has lived here for many years"), and for events at an unspecified past time; past perfect for "a past event or condition completed before another event in the past" ("When I arrived home, he had already called"); future perfect for an action "completed by or before a specified time in the future" ("By next month we will have finished the job"); avoid shifting tense inside a sentence without a reason The tense page is short; the consistency page is longer (about 14,000 characters of text)
S7 Present perfect (read in the browser) British Council LearnEnglish · no date shown Grammar explanation Present perfect links the past to the present; used for an unfinished time period (today, this week, this year), life experience (ever, never), and unfinished states "especially with for, since and how long"; when we say when something happened, we use the past simple Does not explain the difference between for and since (S4 does) and does not list banned time words
S8 Unit 4: Starting and finishing emails (read in the browser) British Council LearnEnglish · no page date (reader comments are dated 2024 to 2026) Lesson Formal emails (to a customer, a manager, a shop, another company) versus informal ones (to a good friend); formal openings "Dear Mr Piper," and "Dear Sir or Madam,"; formal closings "Yours sincerely," "Yours faithfully," "Yours truly," (informal ones are "Rgds," "Cheers," "Bye for now," "See you soon,"); a comma after the opening and closing phrase, and the name on a new line Does not say when to use sincerely versus faithfully, so the lessons do not teach it
S9 GMAT Data Insights GMAC (the exam's owner) · no date shown Official guidance Data sufficiency asks only whether the information is enough, not for the value; the idea of "two peep holes in a sealed box", two views of the same reality, both views look onto the same reality Uses GMAT's A-E answer scale; Infosys uses a 1-2-3-4 scale (S13)
S10 LSAT Logical Reasoning sample questions Law School Admission Council · no date shown Official sample questions with explanations Shows "most strengthens the argument", "an assumption required by the argument" and flaw questions, each with a worked explanation; instructs the test taker to choose the best answer and not to make implausible assumptions LSAT is harder than the Infosys items; used for the vocabulary and the habit of stating the argument's gap, not for difficulty
S11 GRE Verbal Reasoning overview ETS · no date shown Official description Reading comprehension skills include "identifying the author's assumptions and perspective", "identifying strengths and weaknesses of a position", distinguishing major from minor points Lists skills only; it has no "strengthen/weaken" question categories
S12 OpenStax Prealgebra 2e: 5.6 Ratios and Rate · 6.2 General Applications of Percent · 6.5 Proportions OpenStax, Rice University · page footer "Jun 29, 2026" Open textbook sections A ratio compares two quantities in the same unit; a proportion says two ratios are equal; percent problems as an equation Definitions only; every worked number in the lessons is checked in code
S14 Parallel Structure Purdue OWL · ©1995-2018, no page date Reference handout "Parallel structure means using the same pattern of words to show that two or more ideas have the same level of importance"; join parallel items with and/or; do not mix forms (hiking, swimming, to ride is not parallel; hiking, swimming, riding is) —
S15 Dangling Modifiers and How To Correct Them Purdue OWL · ©1995-2018, no page date Reference handout An opening phrase that names an action (e.g. "Having finished the assignment") must be followed by the person who did it as the subject of the main clause; "Having finished the assignment, the TV was turned on" is incorrect because TV sets do not finish assignments —
S16 Using Pronouns Clearly Purdue OWL · ©1995, no page date Reference handout A pronoun agrees in number with its noun (a singular noun takes a singular pronoun); do not shift person (I to you to he); refer to a specific noun, not a vague one (an it that could be the motorcycle or the tree is unclear) The page also discusses the singular they; the lessons avoid questions that depend on it
S17 Email Etiquette Purdue OWL · ©1995, no page date Guidance page Include a meaningful subject line; open with a greeting such as "Dear Dr. Jones,"; use standard spelling, punctuation and capitalisation; write clear, short paragraphs and be direct; typical closings include a sign-off phrase (best, thanks, sincerely) and the sender's name Written for students emailing teachers; the register advice applies to a formal test email
S18 Essays for Exams Purdue OWL · ©1995, no page date Guidance page (about 11,000 characters) Read all the questions; budget your time; underline the key words; plan on scratch paper; begin each answer with a one or two sentence thesis that restates the question as a statement; support it with specific references; proofread for spelling and mechanics Written for written exams in college courses, not for a 10-minute test essay
S19 Oxford Learner's Dictionaries: number Oxford University Press · no date shown Learner's dictionary entry Its own examples show the pattern: "The number of homeless people has increased dramatically" and "A number of (= some) problems have arisen" The rule is read from the dictionary's examples; the page does not state it as a rule
S20 Gerunds, Participles, and Infinitives (Gerunds) Purdue OWL · ©1995-2018, no page date Reference handout A gerund (the -ing form used as a noun) can be the object of a preposition: "The police arrested him for speeding" Does not mention look forward to; that idiom is applied from the rule and tagged [INFERRED]
S21 Concision · Eliminating Words Purdue OWL · ©1995, no page date Reference handouts "The goal of concise writing is to use the most effective words"; concise writing "does not always have the fewest words, but it always uses the strongest ones"; omit redundant pairs (the page lists, among others, past history, terrible tragedy, final outcome, free gift, end result, and says completely finish is redundant); omit repetitive wording Examples are about prose style; the lesson uses them for the common test pattern of an unnecessary phrase
S22 Passive Verbs Purdue OWL · ©1995-2018, no page date Reference handout The passive puts the receiver of the action first and keeps the tense of the active verb: simple present active "The company ships the computers" becomes passive "Computers are shipped"; present progressive "is forming" becomes "is being formed" Examples are in the present tenses; the past and future passives in the lessons follow the same pattern (a form of be in the same tense, then the past participle) [INFERRED]
S23 Basic Ozone Layer Science US Environmental Protection Agency · "Last updated on March 4, 2026" Government science page The ozone layer protects life from the sun's harmful radiation; ultraviolet-B is mostly absorbed by ozone; CFCs, used for refrigeration, air conditioning and aerosol propellants, rise into the upper atmosphere, are broken down by strong ultraviolet radiation and release chlorine that destroys ozone; more UV-B means more skin cancers and cataracts and damage to crops and some marine organisms Used only to check the facts in the model essay
S24 The Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer UN Environment Programme, Ozone Secretariat · no date shown Treaty page The agreement phases out the chemicals that deplete the ozone layer; it was signed in 1987 and entered into force in 1989 Used only to check the facts in the model essay
S13 Infosys's own sample paper (see research/05-source-credibility.md) Infosys Ltd · 2020 First-party The 1-2-3-4 data-sufficiency scale, the pseudocode dialect, the shape of every section 2020, five sections, no answer key
