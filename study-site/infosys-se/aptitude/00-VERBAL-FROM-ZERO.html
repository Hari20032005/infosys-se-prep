Verbal-Ability - From Zero

Adapted from L&T/lt-prep-library/aptitude/00-VERBAL-FROM-ZERO.md; verified in that library's QA. §1 and §2 regenerated for Infosys; the source's crash plan is replaced by ../99-STUDY-PLAN.md.

1. What this section is

In the Infosys test this block is two sections: Verbal Ability (20 Q, 20 marks, 20 min) and English Grammar (5 Q, 10 marks, 10 min, cloze-based). Each has its own cut-off. The English Writing section (one essay or email, 10 min) is taught in W1-english-writing.md.

Conditions that shape every technique in this guide: no calculator (official guidelines), rough paper and stationery allowed, sections are timed and cannot be revisited once closed [LIKELY], and no negative marking has been reported. Answer everything.

The rest of this guide is adapted from L&T/lt-prep-library/aptitude/00-VERBAL-FROM-ZERO.md; its method and speed techniques transfer, its counts and timings do not.

## 3. The order to learn them in

Cheap and high-yield first. Each item below is a topic that either has a fixed recipe (so it is learnable in an hour) or appears often enough to pay for itself immediately.

  1. Coding-decoding. Fixed recipes: letter shifts, letter-to-number sums, reversal. One hour and you are done.
  2. Blood relations. Draw a family tree with symbols; never hold it in your head.
  3. Direction sense. Draw the path on paper and mark each leg. Never solve it mentally.
  4. Number series. Differences, ratios, squares, cubes, alternating patterns. Check differences first, always.
  5. Synonyms and antonyms. Word lists, no reasoning. Pure recall, so it rewards time spent and nothing else.
  6. Percentage, profit and loss. Rests on one table of fraction-percentage equivalents you should memorise cold.
  7. Ratio, proportion, average. Sum-to-ratio and the average = total ÷ count identity. Short and reusable.
  8. Error spotting. Five checkpoints, checked in order. This is the highest-value grammar topic in the section.
  9. Sentence completion. Mostly vocabulary plus context logic; overlaps with topic 5.
  10. Reading comprehension. Two techniques, not knowledge. Learn the method, then practise passages.
  11. Para jumbles. Look for the opening sentence and the pronoun/connector links.
  12. Syllogism. Venn diagrams, drawn small and quickly. Four rules cover almost everything.
  13. Time, speed and distance. Unit conversion is the whole trick. Also underpins trains and boats.
  14. Time and work. Learn the LCM-of-total-work method, not the fraction method.
  15. Data interpretation. Only if you have spare time. Tables are cheap; charts with many bars are not.
  16. Seating arrangement and puzzles. Learn the most-constrained-clue-first method and then practise plenty.
  17. Mixtures and alligation. One focused hour gets you the alligation cross; skip the rest.
  18. Simple and compound interest. Learn the standard formulas and the rough doubling rules. Last.

Why this order: topics 1–5 give you 10–13 questions for about six hours of learning, and they are near-mechanical, so you rarely lose marks to carelessness. Topics 8–12 give you another 8–10 questions for about nine hours. Topics 13–18 are the most expensive per mark and sit at the end for that reason alone.

Do not interleave. Finish a topic before starting the next; the topics do not share skills.

4. The core method for this section

The single most important habit is the fixed exit. Before you attempt anything, decide the exit condition for each question type, and then obey it without negotiation.

Concretely, on the day:

  • Every question gets an answer. No blanks, ever. A guess is worth a quarter of a mark in expectation; a blank is worth zero.
  • Set a 90-second hard cap. When the cap hits, bubble your best current option and move on. Do not "just finish this one" — you are trading a mark you might get for two marks you will definitely lose.
  • Write the clock time on your rough sheet at questions 10, 20 and 30 of this section. Your marks are Q10 at the 9-minute point, Q20 at 18 minutes, Q30 at 27 minutes. Falling behind means it is time to abandon, not to speed up.

The reason this is the core method and not a footnote: with no negative marking, the section rewards coverage and routing, not depth. The candidate who scores 25 here is not the one who solves the hardest puzzle — it is the one who never let a single puzzle eat four minutes.

5. Speed techniques

Quantitative

Option elimination and back-substitution. When options are specific numbers, test the middle one. If a 25% discount question asks for the selling price of a ₹1200 shirt, and options are ₹850 / ₹900 / ₹950 / ₹1000, take ₹900: 1200 − 300 = 900. One multiplication confirms it. You never set up the formula.

Approximation. If a question asks for 19.8% of 505, compute 20% of 500 = 100 and pick the option nearest 100. Options will be spread far enough apart that this works.

Unit conversion, once. Convert to metres and seconds at the very start and never again. A train 200 m long at 72 km/h: 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s, so it crosses a pole in 200 ÷ 20 = 10 seconds. Do the conversion on paper, not in your head.

Fraction-percentage equivalents. Memorise these; they turn most percentage questions into simple division.

Fraction Percentage Fraction Percentage
1/6 16.67% 1/9 11.11%
1/7 14.28% 1/11 9.09%
1/8 12.5% 1/12 8.33%

Logical

Grid building. For seating arrangements, draw the row or the circle and place the fixed elements first. Ten seconds of drawing saves two minutes of confusion.

Most-constrained-clue-first. Do not process clues in the order given. Find the clue that pins an element to an exact position — "C sits third from the left" — place it, and then re-read the other clues against that anchor. The puzzle usually collapses in two or three steps.

Set abandonment. If after 40 seconds you have no anchor position, mark the question, bubble a guess, and leave. Return only if the clock permits.

Verbal

Questions before passage. For reading comprehension, read the question stems first, then hunt the passage for the answer. You do not need to understand the passage; you need to find one sentence. Exception: if the question asks for the main idea, read the first and last sentence of each paragraph and nothing else.

Error-hunting order. Check in this fixed sequence, and stop at the first hit:

  1. Subject–verb agreement ("One of my friends is")
  2. Tense consistency
  3. Pronoun reference and number
  4. Preposition
  5. Article

Most error-spotting questions are solved at checkpoint 1.

6. Twenty practice questions

1. Choose the word closest in meaning to lucid. a) clear b) obscure c) heavy d) brief

2. Choose the word most nearly opposite in meaning to frugal. a) thrifty b) extravagant c) miserly d) careful

3. Spot the error: One of my friend (a) / is going (b) / to Mumbai (c) / tomorrow (d). a) c b) b c) a d) d

4. The committee was ______ in its praise of the new policy, noting several concerns in the same breath. a) unanimous b) effusive c) guarded d) reckless

5. A shirt marked ₹1200 is sold at a 25% discount. What is the selling price? a) ₹850 b) ₹1000 c) ₹950 d) ₹900

6. What is the average of the first 10 natural numbers? a) 5.5 b) 5 c) 6 d) 6.5

7. If 40% of a number is 96, what is the number? a) 200 b) 220 c) 230 d) 240

8. ₹1400 is divided between A and B in the ratio 3:4. What is A's share? a) ₹800 b) ₹600 c) ₹500 d) ₹700

9. Find the next term: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ? a) 42 b) 40 c) 44 d) 36

10. If CAT is coded as 24 (3 + 1 + 20), how is DOG coded? a) 25 b) 27 c) 26 d) 28

11. Pointing to a photograph, a man says, "She is the daughter of my grandfather's only son." How is she related to him? a) Cousin b) Sister c) Aunt d) Niece

12. A man walks 5 km north, turns right and walks 3 km, then turns right and walks 5 km. How far is he from the starting point? a) 3 km b) 8 km c) 13 km d) 5 km

13. A can finish a job in 12 days and B in 18 days. Working together, how long will they take? a) 6 days b) 7 days c) 8 days d) 7.2 days

14. A train 200 m long runs at 72 km/h. How long does it take to cross a pole? a) 8 s b) 12 s c) 10 s d) 15 s

15. An article costing ₹400 is sold for ₹500. What is the profit percentage? a) 20% b) 30% c) 22% d) 25%

16. Statements: All roses are flowers. Some flowers fade quickly. Conclusions: I. Some roses fade quickly. II. Some flowers are roses. a) Only II follows b) Only I follows c) Both follow d) Neither follows

17. Arrange the sentences into a coherent paragraph. Q. Many people believe that multitasking makes them more productive. P. Research, however, suggests otherwise. R. The brain does not run two demanding tasks at once. S. It switches between them, and each switch costs time and accuracy. a) QPSR b) PQRS c) RSPQ d) QPRS

18. The company's revenue grew 20% last year, but its operating margin fell from 15% to 12%. Management attributed the decline to higher raw-material costs and a weaker rupee. Which of the following can be inferred? a) The company sold fewer units than the previous year. b) Revenue growth did not translate into higher profitability. c) Raw-material costs fell during the year. d) The rupee strengthened against major currencies.

19. In what ratio must rice at ₹40/kg be mixed with rice at ₹60/kg so that the mixture costs ₹48/kg? a) 2:3 b) 3:2 c) 3:5 d) 1:2

20. A boat's speed in still water is 10 km/h and the stream flows at 2 km/h. How long does the boat take to travel 24 km downstream and return to the start? a) 4.8 h b) 5.5 h c) 5 h d) 6 h

ANSWER KEY

1. a — Lucid means clear, intelligible.

2. b — Frugal means sparing with money; extravagant is its opposite.

3. c — "One of" takes a plural noun, so it must be "one of my friends."

4. c — "Guarded" fits praise that is tempered by noted concerns.

5. d — 25% of 1200 is 300; 1200 − 300 = 900.

6. a — Sum 1 to 10 is 55; 55 ÷ 10 = 5.5.

7. d — 96 ÷ 0.4 = 240.

8. b — 1400 × 3/7 = 600.

9. a — Differences are 4, 6, 8, 10, so the next is 12; 30 + 12 = 42.

10. c — D=4, O=15, G=7; 4 + 15 + 7 = 26.

11. b — Grandfather's only son is his father; his father's daughter is his sister.

12. a — The north and south legs cancel, leaving 3 km east.

13. d — 1/12 + 1/18 = 5/36; the time is 36/5 = 7.2 days.

14. c — 72 km/h = 20 m/s; 200 ÷ 20 = 10 seconds.

15. d — Profit is 100 on a cost of 400, so 25%.

16. a — "Some flowers are roses" is the valid converse; the fading flowers need not include roses.

17. d — Q opens with the common belief, P counters it, and R–S explain the counter.

18. b — Revenue rose while margin fell, so profitability did not improve proportionally.

19. b — Alligation gives (60 − 48) : (48 − 40) = 12 : 8 = 3 : 2.

20. c — Downstream 12 km/h takes 2 h; upstream 8 km/h takes 3 h; total 5 h.
